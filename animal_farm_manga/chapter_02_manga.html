📖 UNDERSTANDING CHAPTER 2

The chapter in 8 steps

  1. Old Major dies. For three months the pigs secretly teach his ideas, now called Animalism.
  2. Three pigs lead: Napoleon (gets his way), Snowball (ideas and energy), Squealer (persuasive talker).
  3. Some animals resist: loyalty to Jones, not caring, Mollie's sugar and ribbons, and Moses's Sugarcandy Mountain.
  4. Jones falls into drinking and neglect. One weekend the animals are not fed at all.
  5. Starving and then whipped, the animals attack without a plan, and Jones, his men and Mrs Jones flee.
  6. They destroy the tools of control (whips, bits, nose-rings, ribbons) and celebrate.
  7. They keep the farmhouse as a museum, rename the farm Animal Farm, and paint the Seven Commandments on the barn wall.
  8. The pigs milk the cows. Napoleon says "never mind the milk", and by evening the milk has disappeared.

This is a straight comparison of the two texts. The pigs say they "reduced" Major's principles to seven. Notice what was kept and what was left out.

What the book says vs. interpretation

BOOK SAYS The pigs taught themselves to read in secret. The pigs wrote the Commandments. Napoleon stood in front of the milk buckets and said "That will be attended to." He said he would "follow in a few minutes". By evening the milk had gone.

INTERPRETATION Many readers see Moses and Sugarcandy Mountain as Orwell's picture of religion promising a reward after death, which can keep people from fighting for a better life now. Squealer represents propaganda: persuasive talk that can "turn black into white". Snowball and Napoleon are also often linked to real leaders of the Russian Revolution, but I'll explain that later, because it would spoil what happens next.

WORTH NOTICING The Rebellion happened because of hunger, not because of a plan. The pigs are already the ones who read, write the rules, and decide. Keep the missing milk in mind.
