1 · THE HAY HARVEST

HEAVE! HO!
How they toiled and sweated to get the hay in! But their efforts were rewarded: the harvest was an even bigger success than they'd hoped.
Sometimes it's hard. The tools were made for humans, and no animal can use a tool that needs standing on its hind legs.
🧰❌
But the pigs are so clever they can find a way round every difficulty. And the horses know every inch of the field and understand mowing better than Jones ever did.
PigPig
The pigs did not actually work, but directed and supervised the others.
"With their superior knowledge it was natural that they should assume the leadership."
Boxer and Clover harness themselves to the cutter or horse-rake (no bits or reins needed now!) and tramp round and round the field, with a pig walking behind them...
BoxerCloverPig
the pigGee up, comrade! ... Whoa back, comrade!
Every animal, down to the humblest, works. Even the ducks and hens carry tiny wisps of hay in their beaks all day in the sun.
🦆🐔🌾
They finish two days faster than Jones and his men ever did. The biggest harvest the farm has ever seen!
No waste: the hens and ducks pick up the very last stalk. And not one animal steals even a mouthful.
What's happeningThe animals' first harvest is a big success. But notice how the work is divided: the horses and other animals do the physical work, while the pigs give orders. The book says this was seen as "natural" because the pigs knew more.

2 · A HAPPY SUMMER

All summer, the farm runs like clockwork. The animals are happier than they had ever imagined possible.
☀️
Every mouthful of food is a real pleasure, because it's truly theirs: produced by themselves, for themselves, not handed out by a grudging master.
🌾😋
With the "worthless parasitical human beings" gone, there's more for everyone to eat, and more free time too.
There are difficulties. When they harvest the corn, they have to tread it out the ancient way and blow the chaff away with their breath, because the farm has no threshing machine.
PHHOOOO!
Pigs' cleverness+Boxer's muscles
But the pigs' cleverness and Boxer's tremendous muscles always pull them through.

3 · BOXER: "I WILL WORK HARDER!"

Boxer is everyone's hero. Even in Jones's time he was a hard worker. Now he seems more like THREE horses than one.
BoxerBoxerBoxer
Some days the whole farm's work seems to rest on his mighty shoulders.
He arranges with one of the cockerels to wake him half an hour earlier than everyone else.
🐓
COCK-A-DOODLE!
He uses the extra time to do volunteer work wherever it's most needed.
His answer to every problem, every setback:
Boxer
"I will work harder!"
He adopts it as his personal motto.
What's happeningBoxer shows his character: strong, loyal and endlessly hard-working. His answer to every problem is to work harder himself, not to ask why something went wrong.

4 · EVERYONE WORKS... ALMOST

Everyone works according to their ability. The hens and ducks save five bushels of corn by gathering up stray grains.
Nobody steals. Nobody grumbles about rations. The quarrelling, biting and jealousy of the old days have almost disappeared.
Nobody shirked... or almost nobody.
Mollie
Mollie isn't good at getting up in the mornings...
MollieOw, there's a stone in my hoof! I'd better stop early...
Cat
The cat can never be found when there's work. She vanishes for hours, then reappears at mealtimes as if nothing had happened.
PURRR~
But her excuses are so good, and she purrs so lovingly, that it's impossible not to believe her good intentions.
Benjamin
Old Benjamin seems completely unchanged. Same slow, stubborn work as in Jones's time: never shirking, never volunteering for extra. He gives no opinion about the Rebellion.
someoneAren't you happier now that Jones is gone?
BenjaminDonkeys live a long time. None of you has ever seen a dead donkey.
The others had to be content with this cryptic (mysterious) answer.
What's happeningMost animals work hard and get along well. Two don't: Mollie makes excuses, and the cat disappears. Benjamin does exactly his share and no more. His odd answer is a puzzle; the book doesn't explain it. One reading is that he has lived a long time, has seen a lot, and is waiting to see whether things really stay better.

5 · SUNDAYS ON ANIMAL FARM

No work on Sundays. Breakfast is an hour later. Then, every week without fail, there's a ceremony.
First: hoisting the flag. Snowball found an old green tablecloth of Mrs Jones's and painted a hoof and a horn on it in white.
🟩
SnowballGreen for the green fields of England.
🐾
SnowballHoof and horn for the future Republic of the Animals, which will arise when the human race is finally overthrown.
Then everyone troops into the big barn for a general assembly called the Meeting. They plan the coming week's work and debate "resolutions" (proposals).
It is always the pigs who put forward the resolutions. The other animals understand how to vote, but can never think of any resolutions of their own.
Snowball and Napoleon are by far the most active in debates. But they never agree: whatever one suggests, the other can be counted on to oppose.
SnowballVSNapoleon
Even when they decide to set aside the small paddock behind the orchard as a rest home for animals too old to work (something nobody could object to), there's a stormy debate about the correct retirement age for each kind of animal!
The Meeting always ends with Beasts of England. The afternoon is for rest and fun.
♪♪
The pigs set aside the harness-room as their headquarters. In the evenings they study blacksmithing, carpentry and other skills from books taken from the farmhouse.
📚🔨
What's happeningThe farm now has its own symbols (a flag), its own government (the Sunday Meeting), and its own song. Look at who holds the power: only the pigs propose ideas, and the others just vote. Snowball and Napoleon are rivals who disagree about everything.

Snowball

🐄 Clean Tails League (cows)
🐀 Wild Comrades' Re-education Committee (to tame rats and rabbits)
🐑 Whiter Wool Movement (sheep)
...and many others, plus classes in reading and writing.
On the whole, these projects were a
FAILURE.
The wild creatures behaved as before, and when treated generously, they simply took advantage.
The cat joins the Re-education Committee. One day she's seen on a roof, talking to some sparrows just out of her reach.
Cat🐦🐦
CatAll animals are comrades now! Any sparrow can come and perch on my paw~
The sparrows kept their distance.
What's happeningSnowball is full of plans, but most of them fail. The cat scene is a small joke: she uses the language of the Rebellion ("we're all comrades now") to try to catch birds she would normally eat, and the sparrows are smart enough not to trust her.

8 · FOUR LEGS GOOD, TWO LEGS BAD

Snowball
SnowballThe Seven Commandments can be reduced to a single maxim (short rule)...
FOUR LEGS GOOD, TWO LEGS BAD!
This, he said, held the essential principle of Animalism. Whoever fully understood it would be safe from human influences.
🐔🦆🕊️
the birdsWait! We have two legs too!
SnowballA bird's wing, comrades, is an organ of propulsion and not of manipulation. It should therefore be regarded as a leg. The distinguishing mark of man is the hand, the instrument with which he does all his mischief.
🐔❓
The birds didn't understand Snowball's long words, but they accepted his explanation.
All the humbler animals set to work learning the new maxim by heart.
It is painted on the end wall of the barn, ABOVE the Seven Commandments, in bigger letters:
FOUR LEGS GOOD, TWO LEGS BAD
Once they know it by heart, the sheep LOVE this maxim. Lying in the field, they start bleating it...
🐑🐑🐑🐑
"FOUR LEGS GOOD, TWO LEGS BAD! FOUR LEGS GOOD, TWO LEGS BAD!"
...and keep it up for hours on end, never growing tired of it.
What's happeningBecause some animals can't remember seven rules, Snowball shrinks them into one simple slogan. Birds count as "four legs" because a wing is used for moving, not for handling things the way human hands do. The sheep can't understand much, but they can chant the slogan endlessly.

📖 UNDERSTANDING CHAPTER 3

The chapter in 9 steps

  1. The first hay harvest is a record success. The pigs direct; the others do the physical work.
  2. A happy summer: more food, more leisure, and less quarrelling.
  3. Boxer works like three horses. His motto: "I will work harder!"
  4. Mollie and the cat avoid work. Benjamin gives a cryptic answer about donkeys living a long time.
  5. Sundays: a green flag, the Meeting (only pigs propose ideas), and Snowball vs Napoleon always disagreeing.
  6. Snowball's committees mostly fail, but the reading classes succeed, though with very different results for different animals.
  7. Snowball shrinks the Commandments into one maxim, "Four legs good, two legs bad", which the sheep bleat for hours.
  8. Napoleon ignores the committees, takes Jessie's and Bluebell's nine puppies "to educate", and hides them in a loft.
  9. The milk was going into the pigs' mash. Now the windfall apples go to the pigs too. Squealer explains they need them as "brainworkers", or else "Jones would come back!" The animals agree.

What the book says vs. interpretation

BOOK SAYS The pigs "did not actually work, but directed and supervised". Only pigs put forward resolutions. Squealer says milk and apples are needed for the pigs' health, "proved by Science", and that the pigs are "brainworkers". The animals agree because they don't want Jones back. Napoleon took the puppies away "to educate" them and kept them hidden. On the milk and apples, "even Snowball and Napoleon" agreed.

INTERPRETATION This answers the question from the end of Chapter 2 (where the milk went). Look at how Squealer persuades: he names a vague authority ("Science"), says it's for the animals' own good, and then uses fear ("Jones would come back!") so that nobody wants to argue. Compare this with Commandment 7: All animals are equal.

WORTH NOTICING Reading ability matters here. The animals who can't read well, or can't remember the Commandments, have to rely on what others tell them the rules are. Seven rules become one slogan that the sheep chant without needing to think. And keep the hidden puppies in mind.
