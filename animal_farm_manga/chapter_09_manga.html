1 · THE SPLIT HOOF

The day after the victory celebrations, they start rebuilding the windmill yet again. Boxer's split hoof heals slowly, but he won't take even one day off, and makes it a point of honour not to show he's in pain.
Boxer
Only in the evenings does he admit to Clover that it hurts a great deal. She treats it with poultices of chewed herbs.
CloverBoxerBenjamin
CloverA horse's lungs do not last for ever. Work less hard!
She and Benjamin both beg him. Boxer won't listen.
I have one ambition left: to see the windmill well under way before I retire.

2 · THE PROMISE OF RETIREMENT

When the laws of Animal Farm were first made, retirement ages were fixed, and generous pensions agreed:
  • Horses and pigs: 12 · Cows: 14 · Dogs: 9 · Sheep: 7 · Hens and geese: 5
  • A horse's pension: five pounds of corn a day; in winter, fifteen pounds of hay; a carrot or possibly an apple on public holidays
So far, no animal has actually retired. The little field meant for them is now for barley. Rumour says a corner of the large pasture will be fenced off instead.
Boxer
Boxer's twelfth birthday is late next summer.

3 · "READJUSTMENT"

The winter is as cold as the last, and food is even shorter. Rations are reduced again for everyone, except the pigs and dogs.
❄️🥣📉
Squealer
SquealerToo rigid an equality in rations would be against the principles of Animalism. There has been a "readjustment" of rations...
(He never says "reduction".)
Squealer (in a shrill, rapid voice)More oats, more hay, more turnips than in Jones's day! Shorter hours! Better water! Longer lives! More young ones surviving! More straw! Fewer fleas!
They believe every word. Jones has almost faded from memory. They know life is harsh, that they're often hungry and cold and working whenever they're not asleep. But surely it was worse in the old days.
Then we were slaves. Now we are free. That makes all the difference.
...as Squealer did not fail to point out.
What's happeningSquealer's "readjustment" is a soft word that hides a hard fact. He also says equal rations are against Animalism, when the Seventh Commandment says all animals are equal.

4 · THIRTY-ONE PIGLETS

In autumn four sows give birth at about the same time to thirty-one piebald young pigs. Napoleon is the only boar on the farm, so it's easy to guess who the father is.

A schoolroom will be built for them in the farmhouse garden. Until then Napoleon teaches them himself in the kitchen. They're discouraged from playing with other young animals.
New rules: any animal who meets a pig on the path must stand aside, and all pigs may wear green ribbons on their tails on Sundays.

5 · SHORT OF MONEY

The farm needs bricks for the schoolroom, machinery for the windmill, lamp oil and candles for the house, sugar for Napoleon's table (he forbids it to the other pigs because it makes them fat), tools, coal, dog biscuits...
  • Hay and part of the potato crop are sold
  • The egg contract rises to 600 a week, so the hens barely hatch enough chicks to keep their numbers
  • Rations cut in December and again in February
  • Lanterns in the stalls forbidden, to save oil
PigPig
But the pigs seem comfortable, and are even putting on weight.
Late February. A warm, rich smell drifts from the little brew-house. Cooking barley!

No mash appears. On Sunday it's announced that all barley is now reserved for the pigs. Soon the news leaks out: every pig gets a pint of beer a day, and Napoleon gets half a gallon, served in the Crown Derby soup tureen.

7 · THE PRESIDENT

April. Animal Farm is proclaimed a Republic and must elect a President. There is only one candidate.

Elected unanimously.
Snowball
The same day, "fresh documents": Snowball didn't just try to lose the Battle of the Cowshed. He openly led the human side, shouting "Long live Humanity!" And his wounds? Made by Napoleon's teeth.

8 · MOSES RETURNS

In midsummer Moses the raven reappears after several years, unchanged: still doing no work, still telling the same stories from a tree stump.
Moses
MosesUp there, just on the other side of that dark cloud, lies Sugarcandy Mountain, that happy country where we poor animals shall rest for ever from our labours!
Many believe him. Their lives are hungry and hard, so isn't it right that a better world should exist somewhere?
☁️🍬
The pigs' attitude is odd. They say his stories are lies, and yet they let him stay, without working, with a gill of beer a day.

9 · THE WILL TO CONTINUE

His hoof healed, Boxer works harder than ever. All the animals work like slaves: the farm, the windmill, and now the schoolhouse for the young pigs.
Boxer
He never falters. But his coat is less shiny, and his great haunches seem to have shrunk.
the othersBoxer will pick up when the spring grass comes on.
Spring comes. Boxer grows no fatter.
Straining against a huge boulder on the quarry slope, only the will to continue keeps him on his feet. His lips form the words, but he has no voice left:
I will work harder.
CloverBenjamin
Clover and Benjamin warn him again. He pays no attention. His twelfth birthday is near, and he only cares about storing up enough stone before he goes on pension.

10 · BOXER HAS FALLEN

Late one summer evening, two pigeons come racing in.

Half the farm rushes to the knoll. Boxer lies between the shafts of the cart, neck stretched out, unable to raise his head. His eyes are glazed. A thin stream of blood runs from his mouth. Clover drops to her knees.

The animals are uneasy. Except for Mollie and Snowball, no one has ever left the farm. But Squealer convinces them the vet there can treat him better. Half an hour later, Boxer limps back to his stall.

11 · TWO QUIET DAYS

For two days he stays in his stall. The pigs send a big bottle of pink medicine from the bathroom. Clover gives it to him, and in the evenings lies beside him and talks, while Benjamin keeps off the flies.

13 · SQUEALER'S STORY

Three days later it's announced that he died in the hospital at Willingdon, having had every attention a horse could have.
Squealer
Squealer (wiping away a tear)It was the most affecting sight I have ever seen! I was at his bedside. He whispered that his only sorrow was to die before the windmill was finished. "Long live Comrade Napoleon! Napoleon is always right." Those were his very last words, comrades.
Suddenly his manner changes. His little eyes dart suspiciously from side to side.
SquealerA foolish and wicked rumour has been going round. Some of you saw "Horse Slaughterer" on the van and thought Boxer was going to the knacker's. How could anyone be so stupid? Surely you know your beloved Leader better than that!
SquealerIt's simple. The van used to belong to the knacker. The vet bought it, and hadn't yet painted the old name out.
The animals are enormously relieved. He adds details of the death-bed, the admirable care, and the expensive medicines Napoleon paid for without a thought. Their last doubts disappear.
At least he died happy.

14 · THE BANQUET

Napoleon
On Sunday Napoleon gives a short speech in Boxer's honour. His remains couldn't be brought back, but a large wreath of laurels will be placed on his grave, and the pigs will hold a memorial banquet. He ends with Boxer's two mottoes, which every animal should adopt:

"I will work harder"
"Comrade Napoleon is always right"

On the day of the banquet, a grocer's van delivers a large wooden crate to the farmhouse. That night: uproarious singing, then what sounds like a violent quarrel, and at about eleven, a tremendous crash of glass.
CRASH!
No one stirs in the farmhouse until noon the next day. And the word goes round that from somewhere or other, the pigs had acquired the money...
🥃
...to buy themselves another case of whisky.
What's happeningThe book never says where the money came from. It only puts two facts side by side: Boxer goes away in the horse slaughterer's van, and soon after, the pigs have money for whisky.

📖 UNDERSTANDING CHAPTER 9

The chapter in 10 steps

  1. Boxer keeps working on an injured hoof, hoping to retire at twelve.
  2. Rations are cut again for everyone but pigs and dogs. Squealer calls it a "readjustment".
  3. Thirty-one young pigs are born, and pigs get new privileges.
  4. Eggs, hay and potatoes are sold, while barley goes into beer for the pigs.
  5. Weekly "Spontaneous Demonstrations" keep spirits up. Napoleon becomes President, the only candidate.
  6. Moses returns with Sugarcandy Mountain, and the pigs let him stay.
  7. Boxer weakens, then collapses with a failing lung.
  8. A van takes him away. Benjamin reads the words on its side: a horse slaughterer's.
  9. Boxer tries to kick his way out, but is too weak. He's never seen again.
  10. Squealer says he died happily in hospital. The pigs hold a banquet and buy whisky.

What the book says vs. interpretation

BOOK SAYS The van is marked "Horse Slaughterer and Glue Boiler". Squealer says the vet had bought it and not repainted it. The pigs had money for whisky "from somewhere or other". The book does not directly state that they sold Boxer.

INTERPRETATION Almost every reader concludes that the pigs sold Boxer to the knacker and spent the money on whisky, and I think the clues clearly point there. Boxer is usually seen as the loyal working class, whose labour builds everything and who is thrown away when used up. Moses's return is often linked to Stalin easing restrictions on the Church during the war: a promise of a better life after death can make a hard one easier to bear.

WORTH NOTICING Old Major predicted exactly this fate for Boxer in Chapter 1, but under Jones. Also compare the two explanations for Snowball's wounds: in Chapter 7 Jones's shot "only grazed him"; now they are from Napoleon's teeth. Both can't be true.

WORTH NOTICING Literacy matters one last time. Most of the animals were waving good-bye to a van whose sign they couldn't read.
