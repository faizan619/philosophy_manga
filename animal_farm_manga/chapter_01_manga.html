3 · MAJOR'S SPEECH: OUR LIFE

Old Major
Old MajorComrades! I'll tell you about my dream later. First: I don't think I'll be with you for many months longer. Before I die, it's my duty to pass on the wisdom I've gained.
Old MajorWhat is the nature of this life of ours? Let's face it...
MISERABLE.
LABORIOUS.
SHORT.
Old MajorWe're given just enough food to stay alive. Those who can work are forced to work to the last atom of their strength...
...and the moment we're no longer useful, we're slaughtered with hideous cruelty.
No animal in England is free.
The life of an animal is misery and slavery.
"That is the plain truth."
Old MajorIs this simply the order of nature? Is our land too poor?
NO!
Old MajorThe soil is fertile and the climate good. This one farm could support a dozen horses, twenty cows and hundreds of sheep, all in comfort and dignity!
Old MajorSo why do we stay miserable? Because nearly all we produce is STOLEN by human beings. The answer to all our problems is one word...
MAN.
"Man is the only real enemy we have."
What's happeningMajor builds his argument step by step:
  • The problem: our lives are short, hard and hungry.
  • It isn't natural: the farm could easily feed everyone well.
  • The cause: humans take what we produce.

7 · MAJOR'S RULES

Old Major
Old MajorI have little more to say. I just repeat: remember always your duty of enmity towards Man and all his ways.

📜 OLD MAJOR'S TEACHINGS

  1. Whatever goes upon two legs is an enemy.
  2. Whatever goes upon four legs, or has wings, is a friend.
  3. Even after defeating Man, don't become like him. No animal must ever live in a house, sleep in a bed, wear clothes, drink alcohol, smoke tobacco, touch money, or engage in trade. "All the habits of Man are evil."
  4. No animal must ever tyrannise over his own kind. Weak or strong, clever or simple, we are all brothers.
  5. No animal must ever kill any other animal.
  6. All animals are equal.
What's happeningMajor turns his ideas into simple rules: who counts as a friend and who as an enemy, how not to behave like humans, and equality among animals. Rule 3 is a warning: winning against Man is not enough if the animals then copy Man's habits. Tyrannise means to rule others cruelly and unfairly.

What's happeningMajor's long speech is turned into a short, catchy song. A speech is easily forgotten, but a song is easy for every animal to remember and pass on. The song promises a "golden future" but also asks for hard work, "though we die before it break" (even if you die before freedom comes).

📖 UNDERSTANDING CHAPTER 1

The chapter in 6 steps

  1. Drunk Mr Jones goes to bed. The animals secretly meet in the big barn.
  2. Old Major says animal life is miserable, hard and short, and that this isn't natural.
  3. He blames Man, who takes everything the animals produce and gives back almost nothing.
  4. His solution is Rebellion, someday. He gives rules: two legs bad, four legs or wings good; never copy Man's habits; all animals are equal.
  5. He teaches them the song Beasts of England, and the whole farm sings it joyfully.
  6. The noise wakes Jones. He fires his gun into the dark, and everyone scatters to sleep.

What Major's speech does, step by step

StepWhat he says
1. ProblemOur life is "miserable, laborious, and short".
2. Not naturalThe land is rich enough to feed everyone well.
3. CauseMan steals what we produce: "Man is the only real enemy we have."
4. SolutionRebellion, whenever it comes.
5. RulesTwo legs = enemy; never copy Man; all animals are equal.
6. Something to remember it byA song everyone can sing and pass on.

What the book says vs. interpretation

BOOK SAYS The pigs and dogs are "the clever ones" and learn the whole song in minutes. The pigs sit right at the front. The cat votes on both sides. Benjamin "sees nothing to laugh at". Boxer is strong but "not of first-rate intelligence".

INTERPRETATION Orwell wrote Animal Farm (1945) as a fable inspired by the Russian Revolution of 1917. Many readers see Old Major as a figure like Karl Marx or Lenin: an old thinker who dreams of a revolution he won't live to see. Beasts of England works like a revolutionary anthem. This chapter itself never says this; it's background that helps you read the story on two levels.

WORTH NOTICING Major's rules sound simple and fair, especially "All animals are equal" and "don't become like Man". Keep them in mind as you read on, and watch whether the animals follow them.
