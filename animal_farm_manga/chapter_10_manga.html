6 · ONE COMMANDMENT

Benjamin feels a nose at his shoulder. It's Clover, her old eyes dimmer than ever. Without a word she tugs his mane and leads him to the end of the big barn.

What's happening"More equal than others" makes no sense: you are either equal or you aren't. But it says plainly what has been true since the milk and apples. And in Chapter 2 the animals burned the whips; now the pigs carry them.

📖 UNDERSTANDING CHAPTER 10

The chapter in 9 steps

  1. Years pass. Most of the animals who remember the Rebellion are dead.
  2. The farm is richer, but only the pigs and dogs benefit. The windmill is used to mill corn for profit.
  3. The others are hungry but still proud and hopeful.
  4. Squealer trains the sheep in secret.
  5. The pigs walk on two legs; Napoleon carries a whip. "Four legs good, two legs better!"
  6. The Seven Commandments are replaced by one: some animals are "more equal than others".
  7. The pigs wear human clothes and invite the neighbouring farmers in.
  8. Pilkington praises the low rations. Napoleon abolishes "Comrade", the skull, the flag's emblem, and the name Animal Farm.
  9. A quarrel breaks out over cheating at cards, and the animals can't tell pigs from men.

What the book says vs. interpretation

BOOK SAYS The sheep begin bleating "as though at a signal". The title-deeds are in Napoleon's "own possession". The book doesn't say who cheated at cards, or that the pigs physically turned into men. It says that, to the animals watching, it was impossible to tell which was which.

INTERPRETATION Many readers link the dinner to the Tehran Conference of late 1943, when Stalin met Roosevelt and Churchill as an ally. The cheating suggests that the friendship wouldn't last. Orwell dates the book November 1943 – February 1944, the same period.

BACKGROUND In his preface to the 1947 Ukrainian edition, Orwell said some readers might think the book ends in complete reconciliation between pigs and humans. He said that was not his intention: he meant it to end "on a loud note of discord", and wrote it just after Tehran because he did not believe the good relations would last.

WORTH NOTICING Orwell also said in a letter that the turning point of the story is when the pigs keep the milk and apples for themselves (Ch 2–3). The end starts with that small unfairness that nobody stopped.

What the whole book is about

  • Power without checks. Once debates end (Ch 5), nothing can stop the pigs. The problem isn't only that Napoleon is bad, but that no one can question him.
  • Language as a weapon. "Readjustment", "tactics", "without cause", "more equal". Words are bent until wrong sounds right.
  • Memory and literacy. Without records, or the ability to read them, the animals can be told anything about their own past.
  • Fear. "Surely you don't want Jones back?" and the dogs. Fear of an old enemy protects the new one.
  • Loyalty without questions. Boxer's goodness is real, but "Napoleon is always right" lets it be used.
  • Silence of those who understand. Benjamin sees everything and speaks only when it's too late.

These themes are my interpretation, drawn from all ten chapters. Orwell doesn't state a moral directly; he leaves the last picture at the window to say it.
