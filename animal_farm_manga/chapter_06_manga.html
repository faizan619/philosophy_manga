A year of back-breaking work. New rules appear: trade with humans, pigs in the farmhouse, a Commandment that reads differently... and then a storm.

1 · WORKING LIKE SLAVES

All that year the animals worked like slaves.
But they are happy in their work, sure that everything they do is for themselves and future generations, not for "a pack of idle, thieving human beings".
All spring and summer they work a sixty-hour week. In August Napoleon announces work on Sunday afternoons too.
Napoleon
This work is strictly voluntary...
...but any animal who stays away will have their rations cut by HALF.
Even so, some tasks are left undone. The harvest is a little worse than last year, and two fields aren't sown because the ploughing wasn't finished in time. It's clear the coming winter will be hard.
🌾📉❄️
What's happening"Voluntary" work that cuts your food in half if you skip it isn't really voluntary. The animals work harder than ever, but there's already less food.

2 · THE QUARRY

There's limestone in the quarry, and sand and cement in a shed. But how do you break stone without picks and crowbars, when no animal can stand on its hind legs?
🪨❓
After weeks of failure, someone has the idea: use gravity! They tie ropes round huge boulders, and everyone pulls them up the slope (cows, horses, sheep, sometimes even the pigs at critical moments)...
BoxerClover🐄🐑〰️🪨
...and topple them over the edge to shatter on the rocks below!
CRAAASH!
The horses carry the broken stone in carts, the sheep drag single blocks, and even Muriel and Benjamin pull an old pony cart together. By late summer there's enough stone. Building begins, supervised by the pigs.
MurielBenjaminPig (supervising)
It's slow and exhausting. Sometimes a whole day to drag one boulder up, and then it doesn't break. Nothing could be done without Boxer, whose strength seems equal to all the rest put together.
Boxer
When a boulder starts slipping and the animals cry out in despair, it's always Boxer who strains against the rope and stops it. Inch by inch, breath coming fast, hoofs clawing the ground, sides soaked with sweat.
Clover
Boxer, be careful not to overstrain yourself!
Boxer
I will work harder! Napoleon is always right!
He now gets woken 45 minutes early instead of 30, and in his spare moments drags stone to the windmill alone.

3 · THINGS RUN SHORT

The animals aren't badly off. They have no more food than in Jones's day, but no less. Not having to feed five wasteful humans makes a big difference.
And their way of doing things saves work: they weed more thoroughly than humans could, and since nobody steals, they don't need fences.
But as summer goes on, they start running out of things the farm can't produce:
🛢️ 🔩 🧵 🦴 🧲
Paraffin oil, nails, string, dog biscuits, iron for horseshoes. Later: seeds, fertiliser, tools, and machinery for the windmill. How would they get these? Nobody could imagine.

4 · TRADING WITH HUMANS?!

Napoleon
Napoleon (one Sunday)From now on Animal Farm will trade with the neighbouring farms. Not to make a profit, of course, only to get materials we urgently need. The windmill comes before everything. I'm arranging to sell a stack of hay and part of this year's wheat. If we need more money later, we'll sell eggs.
The hens should welcome this sacrifice as their own special contribution to the windmill.
Once again, the animals feel uneasy. Weren't these among the very first decisions made after Jones was driven out?
Never deal with humans... never trade... never use money...?
They all remember passing those decisions. Or at least they think they do.
The four young pigs who protested before raise their voices timidly...
Dogs
GRRRRR!
...and are silenced at once by the dogs' growling.
🐑🐑🐑
"FOUR LEGS GOOD, TWO LEGS BAD!"
As usual, the sheep's chant smooths over the awkward moment.
NapoleonMr Whymper
NapoleonIt's all arranged. No animal needs to deal with humans; I'll carry that burden myself. Mr Whymper, a solicitor from Willingdon, will act as our go-between. He'll visit every Monday for instructions. Long live Animal Farm!
Squealer
Afterwards Squealer goes round the farm calming everyone. There was never a decision against trade and money, he says. It was pure imagination, probably from lies spread by Snowball.
Squealer (shrewdly)Are you certain this isn't something you dreamed, comrades? Have you any record of such a decision? Is it written down anywhere?
It is certainly true that nothing like that was written down. So the animals decide they must have been mistaken.
What's happeningOld Major said in Chapter 1: never "touch money, or engage in trade". Those rules were not put into the Seven Commandments (see the table in the Chapter 2 study notes). Because nothing was written down, Squealer can tell the animals they imagined it.

5 · MR WHYMPER

Mr Whymper
A sly-looking little man with side whiskers. A small-time solicitor, but sharp enough to see before anyone else that Animal Farm would need a middleman, and that the fees would be worth having.
The animals watch him come and go with a kind of dread and avoid him.
But seeing Napoleon, on four legs, giving orders to Whymper, on two legs, makes them proud, and partly reconciles them to the new arrangement.
4 legs2 legs
HumansHumans
The humans hate Animal Farm even more now that it's doing well. In the pubs they prove with diagrams that the windmill will fall down, or that it won't work if it stands.
But, against their will, they start to respect how well the animals run things. They even begin calling it "Animal Farm" instead of pretending it's still the Manor Farm. And they stop supporting Jones, who has given up and moved to another part of the county.
Pilkington❓Frederick
There are constant rumours that Napoleon is about to make a business deal with either Mr Pilkington of Foxwood or Mr Frederick of Pinchfield... but never, it is noticed, with both at the same time.

6 · THE PIGS MOVE IN

Around this time, the pigs suddenly move into the farmhouse. Again the animals seem to remember a decision against this. And again Squealer convinces them otherwise:
Squealer
SquealerThe pigs are the brains of the farm and need a quiet place to work! And it's more fitting for the dignity of the Leader to live in a house than in a mere sty.
(Lately he has started calling Napoleon "the Leader".)
Some animals are disturbed to hear that the pigs eat in the kitchen, use the drawing-room for fun... and also sleep in the beds.
🛏️🐖
BoxerNapoleon is always right.
Wasn't there a rule against beds...?
Clover goes to the barn wall to read the Seven Commandments. She can only read single letters, so she fetches Muriel.
CloverMuriel
CloverMuriel, read me the Fourth Commandment. Doesn't it say something about never sleeping in a bed?
With some difficulty, Muriel spells it out:

4. NO ANIMAL SHALL SLEEP IN A BED WITH SHEETS.

Funny... I didn't remember the part about sheets. But it's there on the wall, so it must have been.
Clover
Squealer "happens" to pass by, with two or three dogs.
SquealerDogDog
SquealerSo you've heard we pigs sleep in the farmhouse beds? And why not? You didn't think there was ever a rule against BEDS, surely? A bed just means a place to sleep. A pile of straw in a stall is a bed! The rule was against SHEETS, which are a human invention. We've removed the sheets and sleep between blankets.
Very comfortable beds they are too! But not more comfortable than we need, with all our brainwork. You wouldn't want us too tired to do our duties? Surely none of you wishes to see Jones back?
The animals reassure him at once. Nothing more is said about the beds. A few days later it's announced that pigs will get up an hour later than the other animals...
...and no complaint was made about that either.
What's happeningCompare the Fourth Commandment in Chapter 2: "No animal shall sleep in a bed." Now the wall says "…in a bed with sheets." The animals can't read well and trust what's written, so they decide their memory must be wrong.

7 · HALF BUILT

By autumn the animals are tired but happy. Food stores for winter are low after selling hay and corn, but the windmill makes up for everything. It's almost half built!
🏗️
Boxer🌕
Boxer even comes out at night to work alone for an hour or two by the light of the harvest moon.
In spare moments the animals walk round and round the half-finished mill, admiring its strong, straight walls, amazed they built something so impressive.
Benjamin
Only Benjamin refuses to get excited. As usual he says nothing except his mysterious remark:
Donkeys live a long time.

8 · THE STORM

November. Raging south-west winds. Building stops because it's too wet to mix cement. Then one night the gale is so violent the buildings rock and tiles fly off the barn roof.
WHOOOOSH!
The hens wake squawking in terror. They had all dreamed at the same moment of hearing a gun go off in the distance.
In the morning: the flagstaff is blown down, and an elm tree at the foot of the orchard has been pulled up like a radish.
🚩⤵️ 🌳⤵️
Then a cry of despair breaks from every animal's throat:
NOOO!
The windmill was in ruins.
🪨🪨🪨
They all rush down. Napoleon, who rarely moves faster than a walk, races ahead of them. There it lies, all their work, levelled to its foundations, the stones scattered everywhere. Nobody can speak.
Napoleon
Napoleon paces back and forth in silence, sniffing the ground. His tail goes stiff and twitches from side to side, a sign that he's thinking hard. Suddenly he stops, as if he's made up his mind.

9 · "SNOWBALL!"

Napoleon
Napoleon (quietly)Comrades, do you know who is responsible for this? Do you know the enemy who came in the night and overthrew our windmill?
SNOWBALL!
Napoleon (roaring like thunder)Snowball has done this! Out of pure spite, to set back our plans and get revenge for being thrown out, this traitor crept here at night and destroyed nearly a year of our work!
Here and now I pronounce the DEATH SENTENCE on Snowball!
"Animal Hero, Second Class" and half a bushel of apples to any animal who brings him to justice. A full bushel to anyone who captures him alive!
The animals are shocked that even Snowball could do such a thing. Everyone starts thinking how to catch him if he comes back.
😠😠
Almost at once, a pig's footprints are found in the grass near the knoll, leading towards a hole in the hedge. Napoleon sniffs them deeply...
NapoleonSnowball's! He probably came from the direction of Foxwood Farm.
Napoleon
NapoleonNo more delays, comrades! This very morning we begin rebuilding the windmill, and we will build all through the winter, rain or shine. We'll teach this miserable traitor he can't undo our work so easily! There must be no change in our plans!
LONG LIVE THE WINDMILL! LONG LIVE ANIMAL FARM!

📖 UNDERSTANDING CHAPTER 6

The chapter in 8 steps

  1. The animals work like slaves: sixty hours a week, plus "voluntary" Sundays that cost half your rations if you skip them.
  2. They learn to break stone using gravity. Boxer does the work of many and adds "Napoleon is always right" to his mottoes.
  3. They run short of things the farm can't make, so Napoleon starts trading with humans through Mr Whymper.
  4. Squealer says the rule against trade and money never existed: "Is it written down anywhere?"
  5. The humans start to respect Animal Farm. There are rumours of a deal with Pilkington or Frederick.
  6. The pigs move into the farmhouse and sleep in beds. The Fourth Commandment now says "…in a bed with sheets".
  7. A November storm destroys the half-built windmill.
  8. Napoleon blames Snowball, sentences him to death, and orders the windmill rebuilt at once.

The rules: then vs now

BeforeNow (Chapter 6)
Major (Ch 1): never touch money or engage in tradeTrade with humans through Whymper. Squealer: "that rule was never passed"
Farmhouse kept as a museum; no animal to live there (Ch 2)The pigs live in it
Commandment 4 (Ch 2): "No animal shall sleep in a bed."The wall reads: "No animal shall sleep in a bed with sheets."
Sunday work stopped (Ch 3)"Voluntary" Sunday work, or half rations

What the book says vs. interpretation

BOOK SAYS The wall now says "with sheets", and Clover "had not remembered" that part. Napoleon blames Snowball for the windmill based on footprints he sniffs. The book doesn't say what really knocked the windmill down. It only describes the violent gale.

INTERPRETATION This chapter shows how rules can be changed quietly: when most of the animals can't read well and nobody kept records, whoever controls the written words controls the "truth". Snowball has become a handy villain to blame for every failure.

WORTH NOTICING Look at who works and who decides. Boxer gives everything. The pigs supervise, trade, move into the house, and get up an hour later.
