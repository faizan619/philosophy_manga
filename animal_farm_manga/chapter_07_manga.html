1 · REBUILDING IN WINTER

A bitter winter: storms, then sleet and snow, then a hard frost that lasts into February. The animals keep rebuilding, knowing the jealous humans will celebrate if the mill isn't finished on time.

The animals "knew" this wasn't so. Still, they build the walls three feet thick this time instead of eighteen inches, which means much more stone.
The quarry fills with snow. It's cruel work. Always cold, usually hungry. Only Boxer and Clover never lose heart.

2 · HUNGER

January. Food runs short. The corn ration is cut drastically, with extra potatoes promised instead...
🌽📉 🥔
...but most of the potatoes froze in their storage heaps because they weren't covered thickly enough. They're soft and rotten. Only a few can be eaten.
🥔❄️
For days at a time the animals have nothing to eat but chaff and mangels (beets).
Starvation seemed to stare them in the face.

3 · FOOLING MR WHYMPER

It's vital to hide this from the outside world. Encouraged by the windmill's collapse, the humans are spreading new lies: that the animals are dying of famine and disease, fighting each other, even eating each other.
Napoleon
If they learn the TRUTH about our food, it could go very badly...
TRICK 1: A few chosen animals, mostly sheep, are told to remark casually within Whymper's hearing...
🐑💬
Our rations have been increased!
TRICK 2: The nearly empty food bins are filled almost to the top with sand, then covered with the last of the grain and meal.
🪣⏳🌾
On some excuse, Whymper is led through the store-shed and allowed a glimpse of the bins.
Whymper
Plenty of food here!
He is fooled, and keeps telling the outside world there's no food shortage on Animal Farm.
These days Napoleon rarely appears in public. He stays in the farmhouse with fierce dogs guarding every door. When he does come out, six dogs surround him and growl if anyone comes too near.
DogDogNapoleonDogDog
Often he doesn't even appear on Sundays. His orders come through another pig, usually Squealer.
What's happeningThe leaders hide the truth from outsiders: the sand in the bins is a literal "cover-up". Napoleon has also become distant. Like a king, he's surrounded by guards and speaks through others.

Squealer

5 · SNOWBALL IS EVERYWHERE

Snowball hasn't been seen. He's rumoured to be hiding at Foxwood or Pinchfield. Napoleon wants to sell a pile of old timber, and both Pilkington and Frederick want to buy it. Napoleon can't decide...
PilkingtonNapoleonFrederick
Leaning towards Frederick? → "Snowball is hiding at Foxwood!"
Leaning towards Pilkington? → "Snowball is at Pinchfield!"
Early spring, an alarming discovery: Snowball is secretly visiting the farm at night! The animals can hardly sleep.
"Snowball"?
They say he steals corn, upsets milk pails, breaks eggs, tramples seedbeds, gnaws bark off the fruit trees...
Whenever anything goes wrong, it's blamed on Snowball. A broken window, a blocked drain...
Snowball did it in the night!
When the store-shed key is lost, everyone is sure Snowball threw it down the well...
🔑
...and they keep believing it even after the key is found under a sack of meal!
🐄🐄 🐀🐀
The cows say Snowball creeps into their stalls and milks them while they sleep. Even the rats are said to be working with Snowball.
Napoleon orders a full investigation. With his dogs, he tours every building, sniffing the ground for Snowball's scent.
NapoleonDogs
Napoleon (in a terrible voice)Snowball! He has been here! I can smell him distinctly!
GRRRRRRR!!
At the word "Snowball", the dogs growl horribly and show their teeth.
The animals are terrified. Snowball seems like some invisible evil force in the very air around them.
What's happeningSnowball becomes the explanation for everything that goes wrong. Note the book's details: his "hiding place" changes depending on which buyer Napoleon prefers, and the animals keep blaming him even after the lost key turns up.

6 · SQUEALER'S "DISCOVERY"

Squealer
That evening Squealer gathers the animals, looking alarmed and making nervous little skips.
SquealerComrades! A most terrible thing has been discovered! Snowball has sold himself to Frederick of Pinchfield, who is plotting to attack us and take our farm. Snowball will guide the attack!
SquealerBut there's worse. We thought Snowball rebelled out of vanity and ambition. We were wrong! Snowball was working with Jones from the very start! He was Jones's secret agent all along. It's all proved by documents he left behind, which we've only just found!
Didn't we see how he tried, luckily without success, to get us defeated at the Battle of the Cowshed?
The animals are stunned. They all remember, or think they remember, Snowball charging ahead at the battle, encouraging them, not stopping even when Jones's pellets wounded his back.

7 · BOXER DOUBTS

Even Boxer, who rarely asks questions, is puzzled. He lies down, tucks his front hoofs under him, shuts his eyes, and with great effort puts his thoughts together.
Boxer
BoxerI do not believe that. Snowball fought bravely at the Battle of the Cowshed. I saw him myself. Didn't we give him "Animal Hero, First Class" right afterwards?
SquealerThat was our mistake, comrade. The secret documents show he was really trying to lead us to our doom.
BoxerBut he was wounded. We all saw him running with blood.
Squealer
SquealerThat was part of the plan! Jones's shot only grazed him. I could show you this in his own writing, if you were able to read it. The plot was for Snowball to signal retreat at the critical moment. And he nearly succeeded! He WOULD have, if not for our heroic Leader, Comrade Napoleon!
Don't you remember? When Jones and his men got into the yard, Snowball turned and fled, and many followed him. And at that very moment, Comrade Napoleon sprang forward crying "Death to Humanity!" and sank his teeth into Jones's leg! Surely you remember that, comrades?
Squealer describes it so vividly that the animals seem to remember it. At least, they remember that at the critical moment Snowball turned to run.
...did that happen?
Boxer
Boxer (still uneasy)I don't believe Snowball was a traitor at the beginning. What he's done since is different. But at the Battle of the Cowshed he was a good comrade.
Squealer (very slowly and firmly)Our Leader, Comrade Napoleon, has stated categorically, categorically, comrade, that Snowball was Jones's agent from the very beginning.
BoxerAh, that is different! If Comrade Napoleon says it, it must be right.
SquealerThat is the true spirit, comrade!
...but it was noticed that he cast a very ugly look at Boxer with his little twinkling eyes.
Squealer
Squealer (turning back)I warn every animal on this farm to keep his eyes very wide open. We have reason to think some of Snowball's secret agents are hiding among us at this moment!
What's happeningSquealer rewrites the Battle of the Cowshed. Go back to Chapter 4: the book's account of the battle never mentions Napoleon at all. It says Snowball charged Jones and was wounded. The animals can't check, because there's no written record and most of them can't read. Boxer is the only one who pushes back, until he's told "Napoleon says so". Notice Squealer's "ugly look" at him.

What's happeningThis is the darkest moment so far. Animals confess to crimes, some of them strange (a dream, a cough), and the dogs kill them on the spot. Notice that Napoleon himself never touches anyone: the dogs do the killing.

9 · ON THE KNOLL

When it's over, all the animals except the pigs and dogs creep away together, shaken and miserable. Which was more shocking: the treachery of those who joined Snowball, or the cruel punishment they had just seen?
Since Jones left the farm, until today, no animal had killed another animal. Not even a rat had been killed.
They go up to the knoll where the half-finished windmill stands, and all lie down together as if huddling for warmth: Clover, Muriel, Benjamin, the cows, the sheep, the geese and hens. Everyone except the cat, who had suddenly vanished just before the assembly.
CloverMurielBenjamin🐄🐑🦆🐔
For a long time, nobody speaks.
Only Boxer stays standing. He shuffles back and forth, swishing his long black tail, whinnying softly in surprise. At last he says:
Boxer
BoxerI do not understand it. I would never have believed such things could happen on our farm. It must be due to some fault in ourselves. The solution, as I see it, is to work harder. From now on I shall get up a full hour earlier in the mornings.
And he trots off to the quarry, drags down two loads of stone to the windmill, and only then goes to sleep.

10 · CLOVER'S THOUGHTS

From the knoll they can see almost the whole farm in the clear spring evening: the long pasture, the hayfield, the pool, the green young wheat, the red roofs with smoke curling from the chimneys, all lit gold by the setting sun.
🌄🌾🏡
Never had the farm, their own farm, every inch of it theirs, looked so lovely.
Clover
Clover looks down the hillside, and her eyes fill with tears.
This is not what we wanted when old Major first stirred us to rebel. If I had a picture of the future, it was of animals set free from hunger and the whip, all equal, each working according to his ability, the strong protecting the weak...
...the way she had protected the lost ducklings with her foreleg on the night of Major's speech.
Clover (Ch 1)🐥🐥🐥
Instead, we've come to a time when no one dares speak their mind, when fierce dogs roam everywhere, and when you must watch your comrades torn to pieces after confessing to shocking crimes.
She has no thought of rebellion or disobedience. She knows they're still far better off than under Jones, and that above all the humans must not return. Whatever happens, she'll stay faithful, work hard, follow orders, and accept Napoleon's leadership.
But still, it was not for this that she and all the other animals had hoped and toiled.
Not for this had they built the windmill and faced Jones's bullets. Such were her thoughts, though she didn't have the words to say them.
At last, as if it could take the place of the words she couldn't find, Clover begins to sing Beasts of England. The others join in. They sing it three times, very tunefully, but slowly and sadly, in a way they had never sung it before.
♪ ... ♪ ... ♪
What's happeningClover is the heart of this chapter. She remembers what the Rebellion was supposed to be: freedom, equality, and the strong protecting the weak. She can see it has gone wrong, but she can't put it into words. She stays loyal, and she sings the old song as a kind of quiet grief.

11 · THE SONG IS BANNED

They've just finished the third time when Squealer arrives with two dogs, looking important.
SquealerDogDog
By special decree of Comrade Napoleon, Beasts of England has been abolished. From now on it is forbidden to sing it.
Muriel
WHY?
Squealer (stiffly)It's no longer needed, comrade. It was the song of the Rebellion. But the Rebellion is now complete. The execution of the traitors this afternoon was the final act. Our enemies, outside and inside, are defeated. The song expressed our longing for a better society in days to come. That society has now been established. Clearly the song has no longer any purpose.
Frightened as they are, some animals might have protested, but at that moment the sheep start their usual bleating...
🐑🐑🐑
"FOUR LEGS GOOD, TWO LEGS BAD!"
...for several minutes, which ends the discussion.

📖 UNDERSTANDING CHAPTER 7

The chapter in 9 steps

  1. A bitter winter. The animals rebuild the windmill with thicker walls while cold and hungry.
  2. Food runs out (the potatoes froze). Napoleon fools Whymper with sand-filled bins and sheep saying rations went up.
  3. Napoleon hides in the farmhouse behind his dogs and gives orders through Squealer.
  4. The hens are ordered to give up 400 eggs a week. They resist, Napoleon starves them, and nine die. The cause of death is covered up.
  5. Snowball is blamed for everything that goes wrong. Napoleon "smells" him everywhere.
  6. Squealer claims documents prove Snowball was Jones's agent all along and that Napoleon was the real hero of the Battle of the Cowshed.
  7. Boxer doubts this, until he's told Napoleon said it. Squealer gives him an "ugly look".
  8. Animals confess to working with Snowball and are killed by the dogs. The dogs also attack Boxer.
  9. Clover grieves that this isn't what they fought for. Beasts of England is banned and replaced by a song praising the farm.

The Battle of the Cowshed: Chapter 4 vs Chapter 7

What the book showed (Chapter 4)What Squealer now claims (Chapter 7)
Snowball led the defence, charged Jones, and was wounded by the gunSnowball was Jones's agent; his wound was "only grazed", part of the plan
Snowball's squeal for retreat was a planned trick to lure the men into an ambushSnowball "turned and fled" to make the animals lose
Napoleon is not mentioned in the battle at allNapoleon sprang forward crying "Death to Humanity!" and bit Jones's leg
Snowball and Boxer received "Animal Hero, First Class""That was our mistake." Napoleon now wears both medals, which he awarded himself

What the book says vs. interpretation

BOOK SAYS "Since Jones had left the farm, until today, no animal had killed another animal." Compare Commandment 6: "No animal shall kill any other animal." The four pigs executed first were the ones who protested against ending the Meetings. The dogs attacked Boxer after he questioned Squealer. Clover thinks: "it was not for this that she and all the other animals had hoped and toiled."

INTERPRETATION Many readers link the confessions and executions to Stalin's "show trials" and purges in the Soviet Union in the 1930s, when people confessed in public to crimes, often linked to the exiled Trotsky, and were executed. Banning Beasts of England because "the Rebellion is complete" shows how a regime can claim the dream has already come true so that nobody keeps asking for it.

WORTH NOTICING Squealer says, "I could show you this in his own writing, if you were able to read it." The animals' poor reading, which we saw in Chapter 3, now lets the past be rewritten. Also notice that the cat disappeared just before the assembly.
