1 · MOLLIE'S SECRET

As winter comes, Mollie becomes more and more troublesome: late every morning, complaining of "mysterious pains" (though she eats very well), and sneaking off to the pool to admire her own reflection.
Mollie💧🪞
One day Mollie strolls into the yard, flirting her tail and chewing hay. Clover takes her aside.
CloverMollie
CloverMollie, this morning I saw you looking over the hedge into Foxwood. One of Mr Pilkington's men was on the other side. I'm almost certain he was talking to you and you were letting him stroke your nose. What does that mean?
Mollie (prancing, pawing the ground)He didn't! I wasn't! It isn't true!
CloverMollie! Look me in the face. Do you give me your word of honour that that man was not stroking your nose?
MollieIt isn't true!
But she can't look Clover in the face. The next moment she gallops off into the field.
A thought strikes Clover. Without telling anyone, she goes to Mollie's stall and turns over the straw with her hoof. Hidden underneath:
🍬🍬🎀🎀🎀
A little pile of lump sugar and several bunches of ribbon in different colours.
Three days later, Mollie disappears.
GONE.
Weeks later, the pigeons report seeing her on the other side of Willingdon...
...pulling a smart red-and-black cart outside a pub. A fat red-faced man in check trousers is stroking her nose and feeding her sugar. Her coat is newly clipped, with a scarlet ribbon round her forelock.
MolliePublican🍬🎀
"She appeared to be enjoying herself, so the pigeons said." None of the animals ever mentioned Mollie again.
What's happeningMollie never cared about the Rebellion, only about sugar and ribbons (remember her questions in Chapter 2?). She leaves Animal Farm for a human who gives her those comforts. She chose an easy life over freedom.

2 · SNOWBALL VS NAPOLEON

January. The ground is frozen like iron. The pigs plan next season's work in many meetings. It's now accepted that the pigs, being cleverer, decide all farm policy, though their decisions must be approved by a majority vote.
That would work fine... except Snowball and Napoleon disagree on EVERYTHING.
SnowballVSNapoleon
More barley!
No, more OATS!
This field is perfect for cabbages!
It's useless for anything except roots!
Snowball
At the Meetings, Snowball often wins the majority with brilliant speeches.
Napoleon
But Napoleon is better at quietly winning support between Meetings, especially with the sheep.
🐑🐑🐑
"FOUR LEGS GOOD, TWO LEGS BAD!"
The sheep bleat it at any time, and they often interrupt Meetings with it, especially at crucial moments in Snowball's speeches.
Snowball has studied old copies of Farmer and Stockbreeder magazine and is full of plans: field drains, silage, fertiliser, even a scheme for animals to drop their dung straight onto the fields to save carrying it.
Napoleon
Napoleon makes no plans of his own. He just says quietly that Snowball's will come to nothing, and seems to be biding his time (waiting for the right moment).

3 · THE WINDMILL PLAN

But no argument is as bitter as the one about the WINDMILL. On a small knoll, the highest point of the farm, Snowball declares:
Snowball🌬️⚡
SnowballA windmill here could run a dynamo and give the farm electricity! Light and heat for the stalls in winter, a circular saw, a chaff-cutter, a mangel-slicer, an electric milking machine!
The animals have never heard of anything like it. They listen amazed as Snowball describes machines doing their work while they graze or improve their minds by reading.
😮😮😮
He gets the details from three of Jones's books, including Electricity for Beginners, and works in an old incubator shed with a smooth wooden floor.
📚🖍️
For hours he draws with chalk held between the knuckles of his trotter, whimpering with excitement. The plans grow into a huge mass of cranks and cog-wheels covering more than half the floor.
⚙️⚙️⚙️⚙️
The others can't understand them at all, but they're very impressed. Everyone visits at least once a day. Even the hens and ducks come, carefully not stepping on the chalk.
Only Napoleon stays away. He has been against the windmill from the start. Then one day he arrives unexpectedly. He walks slowly round the shed, looks closely at every detail, sniffs at the plans once or twice...
Napoleon⚙️💦
...then suddenly lifts his leg, urinates over the plans, and walks out without a word.
What's happeningSnowball dreams of a modern future powered by electricity, where the animals work less. Napoleon shows his contempt for the plan without saying a word.

4 · THE FARM SPLITS IN TWO

Snowball
It will be hard: stone for the walls, sails, dynamos and cables. But it can all be done in a year! After that, we'll only need to work THREE days a week!
Napoleon
What we need now is MORE FOOD. If we waste time on a windmill, we'll all starve to death!
The animals form two sides, each with a slogan:
VOTE FOR SNOWBALL AND THE THREE-DAY WEEK
VOTE FOR NAPOLEON AND THE FULL MANGER
Benjamin
Benjamin is the only animal who takes neither side.
BenjaminWindmill or no windmill, life will go on as it always has... that is, badly.
They also argue about defence. The humans may attack again, and news of their defeat has made animals on other farms more restless than ever.
NapoleonWe must get guns and train to use them!
SnowballWe must send out more pigeons and stir up rebellion on other farms!
The animals can't decide. They always find themselves agreeing with whoever is speaking at that moment.

5 · THE DAY OF THE VOTE

Snowball's plans are finished. At the next Sunday Meeting, the animals will vote on whether to build the windmill. Snowball explains his reasons, though the sheep keep interrupting with their bleating.
Snowball🐑💬
Napoleon
Napoleon (very quietly)The windmill is nonsense. I advise nobody to vote for it.
He sits down again. He spoke for barely thirty seconds and seemed almost indifferent.
Snowball
Snowball leaps up, shouts down the sheep, and makes a passionate appeal!
SnowballImagine Animal Farm when the hard, dirty work is lifted from our backs! Electricity to run threshing machines, ploughs, harrows, rollers, reapers! Every stall with its own electric light, hot and cold water, and a heater!
Until now the animals were evenly split. But Snowball's speech carries them away. By the time he finishes, there is no doubt how the vote will go...
Just then, Napoleon stands up. He gives Snowball a strange sideways look...
Napoleon
...and lets out a high-pitched whimper, a sound no one has ever heard him make before.
"WHHHIIIIII..."

6 · THE DOGS

A terrible baying sound outside... and NINE enormous dogs wearing brass-studded collars come bounding into the barn!
DogDogDog
GRRRAAH!
They rush straight at Snowball. He jumps from his place just in time to escape their snapping jaws, and he's out of the door with the dogs after him!
SnowballDogs
Too shocked to speak, the animals crowd at the door to watch the chase across the long pasture.
😱😱😱
Snowball runs as only a pig can run. He slips! The dogs almost have him! He's up again... one dog's jaws nearly close on his tail, but he whisks it free just in time...
RUN, SNOWBALL!
With a final burst of speed, he slips through a hole in the hedge with only inches to spare... and was seen no more.
Silent and terrified, the animals creep back into the barn. The dogs come bounding back. Where did they come from? The answer is soon clear:
They were the puppies Napoleon had taken from their mothers and raised in secret.
NapoleonDogsDogs
Not yet fully grown, but huge and fierce as wolves. They stay close to Napoleon, and they wag their tails at him the same way the other dogs used to wag them at Mr Jones.
What's happeningThis is the turning point of the book so far. Napoleon couldn't win the vote, so he used force. The nine puppies he hid away in Chapter 3 have become his private guards. Snowball is driven off the farm.

7 · NO MORE DEBATES

Napoleon, followed by the dogs, climbs onto the raised platform where old Major once gave his speech.
NapoleonDogs
NapoleonFrom now on, Sunday-morning Meetings will END. They are unnecessary and waste time. A special committee of pigs, led by me, will decide everything in private and tell you the decisions.
You'll still gather on Sundays to salute the flag, sing Beasts of England, and receive your orders. But there will be no more debates.
Boxer
The animals are dismayed. Even Boxer is troubled. He puts his ears back, shakes his forelock, and tries hard to put his thoughts in order...
...
...but can't think of anything to say.
Four young porkers in the front row squeal in protest and jump up to speak...
PorkerPorkerPorkerPorker
Dogs
GRRRRR...
The dogs give deep, menacing growls. The pigs fall silent and sit down. Then the sheep start bleating "Four legs good, two legs bad!" for nearly a quarter of an hour, which ends any chance of discussion.

8 · SQUEALER EXPLAINS

Squealer
SquealerComrades, I trust every animal appreciates the sacrifice Comrade Napoleon has made in taking on this extra work. Don't imagine leadership is a pleasure! It's a deep and heavy responsibility.
No one believes more firmly than Comrade Napoleon that all animals are equal. He'd be only too happy to let you make your own decisions. But sometimes you might make the wrong decisions, and then where would we be? Suppose you'd followed Snowball and his moonshine (silly dreams) about windmills. Snowball, who, as we now know, was no better than a criminal?
somebodyHe fought bravely at the Battle of the Cowshed.
SquealerBravery is not enough. Loyalty and obedience are more important. And I believe the time will come when we find Snowball's part in that battle was much exaggerated.
Discipline, comrades, iron discipline! One false step and our enemies would be upon us. Surely, comrades, you do not want Jones back?
Once again, nobody can answer that. If debates might bring Jones back, the debates must stop.
Boxer
BoxerIf Comrade Napoleon says it, it must be right.
From then on Boxer has TWO mottoes:
"I WILL WORK HARDER"
"NAPOLEON IS ALWAYS RIGHT"
What's happeningSquealer makes taking away the animals' voice sound like a kindness ("you might make the wrong decision"). He starts changing the story of Snowball's bravery. And he uses the same threat as always: "Jones would come back." Boxer stops thinking for himself and adopts "Napoleon is always right".

9 · THE NEW SUNDAYS

Spring ploughing begins. The shed where Snowball drew his plans is locked, and everyone assumes the plans were rubbed off the floor.
🔒
Old Major's skull, now clean of flesh, has been dug up from the orchard and placed on a stump beside the gun. After the flag goes up, the animals must file past it respectfully.
💀🚩
The animals no longer sit all together. In front, on the platform:
DogSquealerNapoleonMinimusDog
Napoleon, Squealer, and Minimus (a pig with a remarkable gift for writing songs and poems), with the nine dogs in a half-circle around them and the other pigs behind. Everyone else faces them.
Napoleon (gruff, soldier-like)The orders for this week are...
Then Beasts of England is sung once, and the animals go.

10 · "TACTICS, COMRADES!"

On the third Sunday after Snowball's expulsion, the animals are surprised to hear Napoleon announce:
Napoleon
The windmill WILL be built after all.
He gives no reason for changing his mind. He just warns that it will mean very hard work, and rations may have to be cut. The plans are already prepared down to the last detail. It will take two years.
That evening, Squealer explains privately:
Squealer
SquealerNapoleon was NEVER really against the windmill. In fact it was his idea from the beginning! The plans Snowball drew were actually stolen from Napoleon's papers!
somebodyThen why did he speak so strongly against it?
Squealer looks very sly.
SquealerThat was Comrade Napoleon's cunning! He only seemed to oppose it, as a trick to get rid of Snowball, a dangerous character and a bad influence. Now the plan can go ahead without him. This is something called...
TACTICS! TACTICS, COMRADES!
He skips around, whisking his tail and laughing merrily.
The animals aren't sure what the word means. But Squealer speaks so persuasively...
SquealerDogDogDog
...and the three dogs with him growl so threateningly, that they accept his explanation without any more questions.

📖 UNDERSTANDING CHAPTER 5

The chapter in 8 steps

  1. Mollie is caught letting a human stroke her nose, hides sugar and ribbons, and then leaves for a life pulling a cart for a publican.
  2. Snowball and Napoleon disagree about everything. Snowball wins with speeches; Napoleon wins support quietly, especially from the sheep.
  3. Snowball designs a windmill to bring electricity and a three-day week. Napoleon urinates on the plans.
  4. The farm splits: "three-day week" vs "full manger". Benjamin says life will go on badly either way.
  5. At the vote, Snowball's speech wins the crowd. Then Napoleon whimpers, and nine fierce dogs chase Snowball off the farm.
  6. The dogs are the puppies Napoleon took in Chapter 3. Napoleon ends all debates; a pig committee led by him will decide everything.
  7. Squealer defends this: "Loyalty and obedience are more important" than bravery. Boxer adopts "Napoleon is always right".
  8. Three weeks later Napoleon announces the windmill after all, and Squealer says it was always Napoleon's idea: "Tactics!"

How Napoleon took power, step by step

StepWhat he did

What the book says vs. interpretation

BOOK SAYS The dogs wag their tails at Napoleon the way dogs used to wag them at Jones. Squealer says Snowball's part in the battle was "much exaggerated", though Chapter 4 showed Snowball leading and being wounded. The animals accept "tactics" partly because "the three dogs… growled so threateningly".

INTERPRETATION Now that Snowball has been driven out, here is the historical link I held back earlier. Many readers see Napoleon as Joseph Stalin and Snowball as Leon Trotsky: two leaders of the Russian Revolution who disagreed about everything, until Stalin drove Trotsky out of the country in 1929 and later blamed him for everything. Snowball's windmill also echoes plans to modernise Soviet industry. I'm still holding back the other matches (like the neighbouring farmers), because they would hint at what happens next.

WORTH NOTICING Napoleon's dogs replace argument with fear. Each time the sheep's chant drowns out a discussion, someone who wanted to speak is silenced.
