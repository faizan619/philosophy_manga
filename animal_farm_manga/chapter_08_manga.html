4 · THE POEM

Minimus
Minimus writes a poem called Comrade Napoleon. In three verses it...

"Friend of fatherless!
Fountain of happiness!
Lord of the swill-bucket!"

  • compares his calm, commanding eye to the sun in the sky
  • thanks him for everything: full bellies, clean straw, and peaceful sleep
  • says that even a newborn piglet should learn to be loyal, and its first squeak should be "Comrade Napoleon!"
Napoleon approves. The poem is painted on the big barn, on the opposite end wall from the Seven Commandments. Above it, Squealer paints Napoleon's portrait in white.
The portrait
What's happeningNapoleon is being turned into a legend. He's rarely seen, he has grand titles, and even the taste of water is his doing. The poem thanks him for full bellies while the animals go hungry. This is called a personality cult.

📖 UNDERSTANDING CHAPTER 8

The chapter in 10 steps

  1. The Sixth Commandment now says "without cause".
  2. The animals work harder than ever. Squealer reads out great figures, but they'd rather have food.
  3. Napoleon becomes a distant, worshipped figure with titles, a poem and a portrait.
  4. He says he'll sell timber to Pilkington, then suddenly sells it to Frederick. The slogans and the stories flip overnight.
  5. The windmill is finished and named Napoleon Mill.
  6. Frederick's banknotes turn out to be forged.
  7. Frederick attacks and blows up the windmill. Pilkington refuses to help.
  8. The animals drive the men off, at a heavy cost. Squealer calls it a victory; Boxer asks, "What victory?"
  9. The pigs get drunk. Napoleon "nearly dies", bans alcohol, then plans to brew it.
  10. Squealer falls off a ladder by the barn wall. The Fifth Commandment now says "to excess".

What the book says vs. interpretation

BOOK SAYS Squealer was "unaccountably absent during the fighting". Napoleon directed "from the rear", but he was also the only one who did not throw himself flat at the explosion. The animals believed the new slogans because Squealer convinced them "their memories had been at fault". The book does not directly say that Squealer painted the wall.

INTERPRETATION I held these links back until now. Many readers see Frederick as Hitler's Germany and Pilkington as Britain and the other Western powers. The sudden deal with Frederick resembles the Nazi–Soviet Pact of 1939. Frederick's broken deal and attack resemble Germany's invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941, and the Battle of the Windmill the terrible cost of that war.

BACKGROUND Orwell changed one detail before publication. An earlier version had all the animals, including Napoleon, throw themselves flat. He changed it to "except Napoleon" because he wanted to be fair to Stalin, who stayed in Moscow during the German advance.

WORTH NOTICING Only two animals see clearly here. Benjamin understands the blasting powder and the ladder, and says almost nothing. Boxer asks the right question, and then accepts the answer.
