The FoxChapter XXI

That was when the fox appeared.

Good morning.
Good morning… I can't see anyone.
I'm here. Under the apple tree.
Who are you? You're very pretty.
I'm a fox.
Come and play with me. I'm so sad.
I can't play with you. I'm not tamed.
Oh, sorry. What does 'tamed' mean?
You're not from here. What are you looking for?
People have guns and they hunt. Very annoying. They also keep chickens. It's their only good point. Are you after chickens?
No. I'm looking for friends. What does 'tamed' mean?
It's something too often forgotten. It means to create ties.

"To me, you are only a little boy like a hundred thousand others. I don't need you."

"And to you, I'm only a fox like a hundred thousand others. You don't need me."

But if you tame me, we'll need each other. You'll be the only one in the world to me, and I to you.
I think I see. There's a flower… I believe she has tamed me.
On another planet? Any hunters there?
No.
How interesting! Any chickens?
No.
Nothing's perfect.

"My life is dull. I hunt chickens, people hunt me. All chickens are alike, all people are alike."

"But if you tame me, I'll know your footsteps from all others. The others send me underground. Yours will call me out, like music."

"See the wheat? I don't eat bread. Wheat says nothing to me, and that's sad. But your hair is gold. Once you've tamed me, the wheat will remind me of you. I'll love the sound of the wind in it."

Please… tame me!
I'd like to, but I haven't much time. I have friends to find and things to learn.
You only learn what you tame. People have no time now. They buy everything ready-made. But no shop sells friends.
What must I do?
Be very patient. Sit a little way off in the grass. I'll watch you from the corner of my eye. Say nothing. Words lead to misunderstandings.

"Each day, you may sit a little closer."

Better to come at the same hour. If you come at four, I'll begin to be happy at three.
If you come at just any time, I'll never know when to ready my heart. We need rites.
What's a rite?

"It's what makes one day different from the rest. On Thursdays the hunters dance with the village girls. So Thursday is my wonderful day."

So the prince tamed the fox.

And when the time came to leave:

Ah… I shall cry.
It's your own fault. I never wished you harm. You wanted to be tamed.
Of course.
Then you've gained nothing!
I have. Because of the colour of the wheat.
Go and look at the roses again. You'll see that yours is the only one. Then come and say goodbye, and I'll give you a secret.
You're not at all like my rose. You're nothing yet. Nobody has tamed you. My fox was like that. Now he's my friend, and unlike any other.

The roses were very put out.

You're beautiful, but you're empty.

"A passer-by would think mine looked like you. But she alone matters more than all of you. Because it's she that…"

Because she is my rose.
Goodbye.
Goodbye. Here is my secret. It's very simple.
We see well only with the heart. What matters most can't be seen by the eyes.
What matters most can't be seen…

…he repeated, so as to remember.

It's the time you gave your rose that makes her so important.
The time I gave my rose…
People have forgotten this. You mustn't. You are responsible, for ever, for what you have tamed. You're responsible for your rose.
I'm responsible for my rose…

The SignalmanChapter XXII

Hello.
Hello.
What do you do here?
I sort travellers into bundles of a thousand. I send their trains off, some left, some right.
They're in a great hurry. What are they looking for?
Even the driver doesn't know.
Are they back already?
These aren't the same ones. It's an exchange.
Weren't they happy where they were?
Nobody's ever happy where they are.

A third one thundered by.

Are they chasing the first ones?
They're not chasing anything. They're asleep in there, or yawning.
Only the children press their noses to the glass.
Only children know what they're looking for. They give their time to a rag doll, and it becomes very important. If someone takes it, they cry.
They're lucky.

Fifty-Three MinutesChapter XXIII

Good morning.
Good morning.

He sold pills that take away thirst. One a week, and you never need to drink.

Why do you sell these?
They save so much time. Experts have worked it out. Fifty-three minutes a week.
And what do people do with the fifty-three minutes?
Whatever they like.
If I had fifty-three minutes to spare, I'd walk very slowly toward a fountain.

End of Episode 7

What is it really saying?

Before opening these, decide for yourself what each chapter was telling you. Then compare. These are readings, and you're free to disagree with them.

Chapter XXI: the fox

A thing becomes precious because of what you give it: time, patience, care. And what you have made ties with, you must answer for.

Where you see it
A little closer each day, "because it's she that…", and the last line.
What the author says
To tame is to create ties. Ties make one fox unlike a hundred thousand. The secret: we see well only with the heart, and it's the time given to the rose that makes her important.
Interpretation
This answers the crying in Chapter XX. The prince had asked whether his rose was rare. The fox changes the question. She isn't unique because there's no other like her. She is unique because she is his. He also learns that love gives even as it hurts: the wheat will mean something for ever.
A fair objection
Time alone doesn't make a thing worth keeping. People also give years to what harms them, and "I've given so much" can trap them there. "For ever" is a heavy word too: responsibility for someone shouldn't mean never being able to leave. And the prince is unkind to the roses. Each of them could be somebody's rose.
Easy to miss
The fox asks for slowness and rites: the same hour, a little nearer, few words. He makes love a practice rather than a feeling. The flower and the prince had the feeling, and no practice.
Ask yourself: whom have you tamed, and who has tamed you? Are you giving them time, or only affection?
Chapter XXII: the trains

Going faster doesn't help if you don't know what you're going to.

Where you see it
"Nobody's ever happy where they are", the child at the window, and the rag doll.
What the author says
People rush both ways without knowing why. They sleep or yawn. Only children look out, and only children know what they're looking for.
Interpretation
The rag doll is the rose again: a plain thing made important by time. The adults' trouble isn't travel. It's that nothing out of the window has been given any of their attention.
A fair objection
Wanting to move is also how people find better work, safety and love. Being unhappy where you are is sometimes good information. And tired commuters asleep on a train have often earned it. The useful question is whether you chose the direction.
Ask yourself: where is your train going, and did you choose it? When did you last look out of the window?
Chapter XXIII: the pill

Saving time is only worth something if you know what you're saving it for.

Where you see it
"Whatever they like" and the walk to the fountain.
What the author says
A pill saves fifty-three minutes a week by removing thirst. The prince would spend them walking slowly to a fountain.
Interpretation
The pill takes away the need, and with it the pleasure of meeting the need. Walking to water isn't lost time for the prince. It's what the time is for.
A fair objection
Saving time has freed people from a great deal of hard labour. In places where someone walks hours each day for water, the pill would be a blessing, and calling the walk charming would be cruel. The chapter works best as a question about what we do with the minutes, and less as a case against inventions.
Ask yourself: what did your last time-saver save you, and where did those minutes actually go?
The thread through these three chapters

The fox spends time, the travellers race it, the shopkeeper saves it. Only the fox gets something from it.

Look back
The six planets were full of people giving their time to counting, commanding and drinking. Here is the missing idea: time given to someone is what makes them matter. Compare it with the businessman's "I own".
Watch for
The prince now knows he is responsible for a rose who is very far away. See what he does about it. The last episode covers the end of the book, so read Chapters XXIV to XXVII first.
