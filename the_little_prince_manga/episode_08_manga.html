A Well SomewhereChapter XXIV

It was the eighth day since my breakdown. I had listened to the shopkeeper's story while drinking my last drop of water.

Your memories are lovely. But I haven't mended my plane, and there's nothing to drink. I'd be happy to walk to a fountain too!
My friend the fox…
This has nothing to do with the fox!
Why?
Because we're going to die of thirst.
It's good to have had a friend, even if you're going to die. I'm very glad I had a fox for a friend.
He can't judge the danger. He's never hungry or thirsty.
I'm thirsty too. Let's look for a well.

It's absurd to look for a well at random in an endless desert. We set off all the same.

We walked for hours without a word. Night fell, and the stars came out.

So you're thirsty too?
Water can be good for the heart as well.

I didn't understand. I knew better than to question him.

He was tired. We sat down.

The stars are beautiful because of a flower we can't see.
What makes the desert beautiful is that it hides a well somewhere.

I understood at once. As a boy I lived in an old house. They said treasure was buried there. Nobody ever found it, but it enchanted the whole house.

Yes. A house, the stars, the desert: what makes them beautiful can't be seen.
I'm glad you agree with my fox.

He fell asleep. I took him in my arms and walked on. It was like carrying a fragile treasure. Nothing on Earth seemed more fragile.

"What I see is only a shell. What moves me is his loyalty to a flower. It shines in him like the flame of a lamp, even in sleep."

Lamps must be sheltered. A gust can blow them out. I walked on, and at daybreak I found the well.

The WaterChapter XXV

People pile into express trains, and no longer know what they want. So they fuss, and go in circles. It isn't worth it.

This wasn't like a Sahara well. It was like a village well. But there was no village. I thought I was dreaming.

How strange. It's all ready: the pulley, the bucket, the rope.

He laughed, and worked the pulley. It creaked like an old weathervane.

Do you hear? We've woken the well, and it's singing.
Let me. It's too heavy for you.

Slowly I raised the bucket and set it on the edge.

In the water, still trembling, I saw the sun tremble.

I'm thirsty for this water. Let me drink.

And I understood what he'd been looking for. He drank with his eyes closed. It was sweet as a feast.

This water was born of the walk under the stars, the song of the pulley, the work of my arms. It was good for the heart, like a gift.

When I was small, the lights on the tree, the midnight music and the kind smiles made my present shine in the same way.

People here grow five thousand roses in one garden, and don't find what they're looking for. Yet it could be found in one rose, or a little water. The eyes are blind. You have to look with the heart.

I had drunk. I could breathe. Why, then, did I feel sad?

You must keep your promise. A muzzle for my sheep. I'm responsible for that flower!
Your baobabs look like cabbages! And your fox's ears look like horns!
You're unfair! I could only draw boas.
It's all right. Children understand.

I drew a muzzle. And when I gave it to him, my heart tightened.

You have plans I don't know about.

He didn't answer.

You know, tomorrow it's a year since I fell to Earth. It was quite near here.
So it wasn't by chance, that morning, that you were walking here alone? You were going back to where you fell?

He blushed again. And when people blush, it means yes, doesn't it?

Ah. I'm afraid.
You must work now. Go back to your machine. I'll wait for you here. Come tomorrow evening.

But I wasn't reassured. I remembered the fox. If you let yourself be tamed, you risk a few tears.

I Won't Leave YouChapter XXVI

Beside the well stood an old stone wall. Next evening I saw him from far off, sitting up there. And I heard him speak.

Don't you remember? It isn't quite here… Yes, it's the right day. But not the right place.
You'll see where my footprints start in the sand. Wait for me there. I'll come tonight.

I still saw nobody.

Your poison is good? You're sure I won't suffer long?

I stopped, my heart torn. I didn't understand.

Now go away. I want to get down.

Then I looked at the foot of the wall.

It was one of those that can end your life in thirty seconds. I ran. At the noise, it slid into the sand like a dying jet of water, and was gone between the stones.

I reached the wall just in time to catch him. He was white as snow.

What's this? You talk to snakes now?

He put his arms round my neck. His heart beat like a dying bird's.

I'm glad you found what was wrong with your engine. You can go home now.

How did he know? I had come to tell him that I'd mended it.

I'm going home today, too. It's much farther. It's much harder.
I have your sheep. And the box. And the muzzle.

He smiled sadly.

You were afraid, little fellow.
I'll be more afraid tonight.
I want to hear you laugh again.
Tonight makes a year. My star will be just above the place where I fell.
Tell me it's a bad dream, all this about a snake, and a star.

He didn't answer.

What matters can't be seen. If you love a flower on a star, it's sweet to look at the sky at night. All the stars are in flower.
My star is too small to show you. It's better so. It will be one among the stars. So you'll love to look at all of them.

"But all those stars are silent."

Yours will be like nobody else's. I'll be laughing on one of them. So for you, it will be as if all the stars were laughing.

"It will be as if I'd given you, instead of stars, a great many little bells that know how to laugh."

"When you're comforted (we always are, in the end) you'll be glad you knew me. You'll open your window and laugh. Your friends will think you're mad."

Tonight… don't come.
I won't leave you.
I'll seem to be in pain. I'll seem to be dying. It's not worth seeing.
I won't leave you.

I didn't see him leave. He slipped away without a sound. When I caught up, he was walking fast, quite determined.

Ah. You're here.

And he took my hand.

It will hurt you. I'll look as if I'm dead, and it won't be true. It's too far. I can't take this body. It's too heavy.

I said nothing.

It will be like an old shell left behind. Old shells aren't sad. I'll look at the stars too. They'll all be wells with rusty pulleys. They'll all give me water.

And he too was silent, because he was crying.

He sat down, because he was afraid.

My flower… I'm responsible for her. She's so weak, and so simple. She has four thorns, worth nothing, against the whole world.

I sat down too. I couldn't stand any longer.

There… That's all.

He hesitated a little, then got up. He took a step. I could not move.

He stood still for a moment. He didn't cry out. He fell gently, as a tree falls. There wasn't even a sound, because of the sand.

Look at the SkyChapter XXVII

That was six years ago. I've never told this story. My friends were glad to see me alive. I was sad, but I told them, "It's tiredness."

Now I'm a little comforted. That is, not quite. But I know he went back to his planet. At daybreak I didn't find his body.

At night I love to listen to the stars. They're like five hundred million little bells.

But something extraordinary has happened. On the muzzle I drew, I forgot the leather strap. He could never have fastened it.

So I wonder: what happened on his planet? Perhaps the sheep has eaten the flower…

Sometimes I say, "Surely not! He covers her every night, and watches his sheep." Then I'm happy, and all the stars laugh softly.

And sometimes: "You only have to forget once. One night he forgot the globe, or the sheep got out without a sound." Then the bells all turn to tears.

For you who love the little prince as I do, nothing in the universe is the same if somewhere, we don't know where, a sheep we don't know has, yes or no, eaten a rose.

Look at the sky. Ask yourself: has the sheep eaten the flower, or not? You'll see how everything changes. And no grown-up will ever understand how much it matters.

To me this is the loveliest and the saddest landscape in the world. It's here that he appeared on Earth, and then was gone.

If you pass this way, don't hurry. Wait a little, just under the star. If a child comes who laughs, whose hair is gold, who never answers questions, you'll know who he is. Then be kind. Don't leave me so sad. Write to me quickly to say he's back…

End of Episode 8

What is it really saying?

Before opening these, decide for yourself what each chapter was telling you. Then compare. These are readings, and you're free to disagree with them.

Chapter XXIV: the hidden well

Things are beautiful because of what they hide, and you find it only by setting out.

Where you see it
The well you can't see, the treasure under the house, and the sleeping child.
What the author says
The stars are beautiful because of a flower, the desert because of a well, the old house because of its secret. The pilot looks at the prince and sees only a shell. What moves him is the prince's loyalty.
Interpretation
The sheep in the box, the elephant in the snake, the well in the sand: it's the first idea of the book, grown up. The pilot who said "I can't see sheep through boxes" now sees a rose shining inside a sleeping boy. He has become the kind of grown-up the dedication hoped for.
A fair objection
A desert can also hide nothing. They were lucky, and many who walk into one aren't. Hope isn't evidence. But notice that the pilot calls the search absurd and goes anyway, because sitting still was certain to fail.
Ask yourself: what hidden thing makes your own ordinary place beautiful to you?
Chapter XXV: the water

What you have worked for and waited for, with someone, feeds more than your thirst.

Where you see it
The singing pulley, the drink, and where the water came from.
What the author says
The water was good because of the walk, the pulley and the effort. People grow five thousand roses and don't find what could be found in one, or in a little water. The eyes are blind. Look with the heart.
Interpretation
This answers the shopkeeper's pill without arguing. The pill removes thirst. The well turns thirst into a shared morning. The fox's lesson, time makes a thing matter, has passed from fox to prince to pilot.
A fair objection
The heart can be blind too. It misreads people it wants to love and fears people it doesn't know. The pilot's eyes and hands mended the engine. A better rule may be to look with both, and to know which one you're using. And effort doesn't always make a thing good.
Easy to miss
Joy and dread come together. Just after drinking, his heart tightens. He remembers what the fox said: letting yourself be tamed risks tears.
Ask yourself: what have you received lately without the walk? What would the walk have added?
Chapter XXVI: the goodbye

You can't always keep the one you love. You can stay beside them, and what they gave you stays with you.

Where you see it
"I won't leave you", the hand, and the bells.
What the author says
The prince has arranged for the snake to send him home. He is afraid. He gives the pilot a present: stars that laugh. He says his body is only a shell. He goes because he is responsible for his flower.
Interpretation
The pilot once answered tears with words he didn't believe. Now he says almost nothing and stays. And the prince, who ran from his flower, goes back to her. Every promise in the book is kept here: the muzzle, the rose, the friend. The gift of the stars is the fox's wheat again: something that meant nothing will now mean someone.
Two readings
Some read this as a death, told in a way a child can bear. Others read it as a journey home, as the prince says. The book keeps both open on purpose.
A fair objection
Please don't take the prince's way as advice. He says his body is too heavy and that leaving won't really be dying. That belongs to a fable about a boy from a star. In real life, those left behind lose a person, not a shell, and being responsible for someone is a reason to stay. The pilot's way is the one to follow: don't leave a frightened person alone.
If this feels close
If you or someone you know is thinking about not being here, please talk to someone today. In India, Tele-MANAS is free at any hour on 14416. Elsewhere, a local crisis line or a person you trust.
Ask yourself: who stayed beside you when there was nothing to fix? Have you done that for anyone?
Chapter XXVII: the strap

To love someone far away is to live with not knowing. And what you love decides how the whole world looks.

Where you see it
The forgotten strap, the laughing stars and the tears.
What the author says
He is comforted, but not quite. He forgot the strap, and so he'll never know. On some nights the stars laugh, on others they weep. He asks us to look up, and to write if the child comes back.
Interpretation
Nothing in the sky changes. What changes is what he believes about one rose. It's the prince's own speech in Chapter VII, now lived by the pilot. And his worry is a kind of love: a sheep we don't know, a rose we've never seen, and we care.
A fair objection
The last jab, that no grown-up will understand, isn't quite true, and the book proves it. A grown-up wrote it, and he dedicated it to one. Perhaps it's a dare.
Easy to miss
He says he didn't find the body and takes it as proof that the prince went home. It's also what a grieving man would choose to believe. The book lets him.
Ask yourself: look at the sky tonight. Has the sheep eaten the flower? What does your answer say about you today?
The thread through the ending

Love makes the world larger and you more breakable. The book thinks it's worth it.

Look back
A boy stopped drawing because nobody saw his elephant. A man stopped talking because nobody wanted the stars. Then someone asked him for a sheep. By the end he draws again, tells the story, and looks up.
