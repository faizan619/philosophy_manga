Something SeriousChapter VII

The fifth day. He asked it suddenly, as if after long, silent thought.

If sheep eat bushes, do they eat flowers too?
Sheep eat whatever they find.
Even flowers with thorns?
Yes. Even those.
Then what are thorns for?

I had no idea. A bolt was jammed, my water was running out, and I was afraid. He never let go of a question once he'd asked it.

Thorns are no use at all! Flowers grow them out of spite!
Oh!

A silence. Then, bitterly:

I don't believe you. Flowers are weak. They protect themselves as best they can. They think their thorns make them terrifying.
I don't believe anything! I said whatever came into my head. Can't you see I'm busy with something serious?
Something serious! You talk just like the grown-ups!

That made me a little ashamed.

"I know a planet with a red-faced man on it. He has never smelled a flower. He has never looked at a star. He has never loved anyone. He has only ever done sums."

I'm a serious man! I'm a serious man!
But he isn't a man. He's a mushroom!
A what?
A mushroom!
Flowers have been growing thorns for millions of years. Sheep have been eating them anyway. And it isn't serious to wonder why they go to the trouble?

"Suppose I know a flower that exists nowhere else, only on my planet. And one morning a little sheep wipes her out in a single bite, without even noticing. That isn't important?"

"If you love one flower, and she lives on one of millions and millions of stars, it's enough to look up at them. You think: she's out there somewhere."

"But if the sheep eats her, it's as though all the stars had gone out."

Night had fallen. I had let go of my tools. The bolt, the thirst, death: none of it mattered.

On one star, one planet, mine, the Earth, there was a little prince who needed comforting.

The flower you love isn't in danger. I'll draw your sheep a muzzle. I'll draw a railing for your flower. I'll…

I didn't know what to say. I didn't know how to reach him. The land of tears is a mysterious place.

The FlowerChapter VIII

Simple flowers had always grown on his planet. They took up no room. They came in the morning and were gone by evening.

Then a seed blew in from who knows where. He watched this shoot closely. It might have been a new kind of baobab.

But it stopped growing and began to make a bud. She chose her colours with care and dressed slowly, adjusting her petals one by one.

She did not want to come out crumpled like a poppy. Her mysterious toilette lasted days and days.

And one morning, exactly at sunrise, she showed herself.

Ah… I've only just woken up. My hair isn't done.
How beautiful you are!
Yes, aren't I? I was born with the sun.

She wasn't too modest. But she was so moving!

It must be breakfast time. Would you be so kind as to think of me?

Embarrassed, he fetched fresh water.

Let the tigers come with their claws!
There are no tigers here. And tigers don't eat grass.
I am not grass.
Forgive me…
Tigers don't scare me. But I can't bear draughts! Do you have a screen?
And put me under glass at night. It's cold here. Where I come from…

She stopped. She had come as a seed. She knew nothing of other places.

Hem, hem… So, that screen?
I was about to get it, but you were talking to me!

She coughed harder, to make him feel guilty.

So, for all the goodwill of his love, he came to doubt her. He had taken unimportant words seriously, and it made him very unhappy.

I shouldn't have listened to her. You should never listen to flowers. You should look at them and breathe them in.

"Mine perfumed my whole planet, and I didn't know how to enjoy it. That talk of claws annoyed me so. It should have filled me with tenderness."

I should have judged her by what she did, and not by what she said. She perfumed and lit up my life. I should never have run away. I should have guessed the tenderness behind her poor little tricks.
But I was too young to know how to love her.

Try to Be HappyChapter IX

On the morning he left, he put his planet in order. He swept out his two active volcanoes. They were very handy for heating breakfast.

He had an extinct one too. He swept it all the same.

You never know!

A little sadly, he pulled the last baobab shoots. He thought he'd never be back, and every ordinary chore felt very dear that morning.

And when he watered the flower one last time, he found he wanted to cry.

Goodbye.

She didn't answer.

Goodbye…

She coughed. But it wasn't because of her cold.

I've been silly. Forgive me. Try to be happy.

No reproaches. He stood there lost, the glass globe in the air. He didn't understand this quiet sweetness.

Yes, I love you. You never knew it, through my fault. It doesn't matter. But you were just as silly as I was.
Try to be happy. Put that globe away. I don't want it any more.
But the wind…
The cool night air will do me good. I'm a flower.
But the animals…
I must put up with a few caterpillars if I want to know the butterflies. Who else will visit me? You'll be far away.
As for big beasts, I'm not afraid. I have my claws.

And, quite innocently, she showed her four thorns.

Don't hang about like this. It's annoying. You decided to go. So go!

For she didn't want him to see her cry. She was such a proud flower.

I think he made use of a flock of migrating birds to get away.

End of Episode 3

What is it really saying?

Before opening these, decide for yourself what each chapter was telling you. Then compare. These are readings, and you're free to disagree with them.

Chapter VII: something serious

What you love is serious, however small it looks to others. Being busy can make you stop seeing it.

Where you see it
The pilot's shadow wearing a hat (this manga's image), then the sky with stars and without.
What the author says
A man who has only ever done sums and loved nobody calls himself serious. The prince calls him a mushroom. Love one flower, and every star means something. Lose her, and they all go dark.
Interpretation
The pilot gives a careless answer to a question that was really about fear: will the one I love be safe? The thorns are the key. They don't work, and a weak creature grows them anyway. The chapter turns when he stops explaining and simply holds him.
A fair objection
The pilot's work is serious. Without that bolt he may die of thirst. Contempt for practical work is easy when someone else does it. The better point is that urgency doesn't excuse cruelty: he could have said "I'm afraid, let me fix this, then I'll listen."
Ask yourself: who asked you something lately while you were "busy"? What might they really have been asking?
Chapter VIII: the flower

Love speaks badly sometimes. Look at what a person does, as well as at what they say.

Where you see it
The perfume he didn't enjoy, what he realises, and the last line.
What the author says
She was vain and difficult, and was caught starting a silly lie. He took careless words seriously, grew unhappy and ran away. Later he sees tenderness behind her tricks.
Interpretation
Both were new to love. She couldn't ask for care directly, so she coughed. He gave everything she demanded and understood nothing she meant. He blames himself, but as someone who has grown.
A fair objection
This can be misread as "put up with anything". Coughing to make someone feel guilty is also an action, and by that measure she wronged him. Tenderness behind manipulation explains it without making it fine. We also hear only his side, told with regret, which softens memory.
Background, not from the story
Many readers connect the flower with the author's wife, Consuelo, and their stormy marriage. It's a common reading, and the book doesn't say so.
Ask yourself: is there someone whose words annoyed you while their actions kept showing care? Or the reverse: kind words, and actions that hurt?
Chapter IX: the goodbye

Pride hides love until it's almost too late. Letting someone go can be the clearest way of saying it.

Where you see it
Her apology, the caterpillars, and the tears he doesn't see.
What the author says
She apologises, says she loves him, and says he was as silly as she was. She refuses the globe. She sends him off sharply so he won't see her cry.
Interpretation
The flower who couldn't bear a draught now chooses the wind. She accepts caterpillars for the sake of butterflies: anything worth meeting comes with discomfort. And ordinary chores become precious only when he thinks they're the last.
A fair objection
Is she strong, or only acting strong? Four thorns won't stop a beast. Her order to leave is kind, and it's also her pride again: one honest "stay" might have changed everything. Neither of them says what they want.
Ask yourself: what is a thing you've left unsaid because of pride? Which of your daily chores would you miss if today were the last?
The thread through these three chapters

Thorns. The weak protect themselves with whatever they have: thorns, coughs, pride, sums, a hammer. Underneath is fear.

Look back
The flower has thorns, the pilot has a temper, the red-faced man has his sums, and the prince has his leaving. The chapters keep asking you to look past the defence to the one behind it. Treat this as a working idea and test it as you read on.
About the order
The book mentions the birds at the start of Chapter IX. This manga shows them last, when he actually leaves.
