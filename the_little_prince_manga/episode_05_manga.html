The BusinessmanChapter XIII

The fourth planet belonged to a businessman. He was so busy that he didn't even look up.

Good morning. Your cigarette has gone out.
Three and two, five… five and seven, twelve… Good morning… twenty-two and six… no time to relight it… thirty-one. Phew! That makes 501,622,731.
Five hundred million what?
Eh? You're still here? I've so much work! I'm a serious man, I am!
Five hundred and one million what?

Never in his life had he let go of a question once he'd asked it.

"I've been disturbed three times in forty-four years. First, 22 years ago, by a beetle. I made four mistakes."

"Second, 11 years ago, by rheumatism. I get no exercise. I've no time for strolling."

"And the third time is now! Where was I? Five hundred and one million…"

Million what?
Those little things you sometimes see in the sky.
Flies? Bees?
No, no! Shiny ones. They set idle people dreaming. I've no time to dream.
Oh! The stars!
What do you do with them?
Nothing. I own them.
But I met a king who…
Kings don't own. They reign over. Quite different.
What use is it to own the stars?
It makes me rich.
And what use is being rich?
To buy more stars, if anyone finds any.

"He reasons a bit like the drinker," thought the prince.

How can anyone own the stars?
Whose are they?
I don't know. Nobody's.
Then they're mine. I thought of it first.
A diamond nobody owns is yours. So is an island. An idea, you patent. And nobody ever thought of owning the stars.
And what do you do with them?
I manage them. I count them, and count them again.
But you can't pick them, or wear them.
No, but I can put them in the bank. I write the number on a slip of paper and lock it in a drawer.
And that's all?
It's enough!
It's amusing. Rather poetic, even. But not very serious.

He had very different ideas from grown-ups about what is serious.

"I own a flower, and I water her every day. I own three volcanoes, and I sweep them every week, even the extinct one."

It's useful to my volcanoes and to my flower that I own them. But you're no use to the stars.
Grown-ups really are quite extraordinary.

The LamplighterChapter XIV

The fifth planet was very strange, and the smallest of all. There was just room for a street lamp and a lamplighter.

He may be absurd. But less than the king, the swank, the businessman and the drinker. His work has meaning, at least.

When he lights his lamp, it's as if one more star is born, or a flower. When he puts it out, they go to sleep. It's useful because it's beautiful.

Good morning. Why did you just put out your lamp?
Orders. Good morning.
What are the orders?
To put out my lamp. Good evening.

And he lit it again.

But why did you light it again?
Orders.
I don't understand.
There's nothing to understand. Orders are orders. Good morning.

He put it out, and mopped his brow with a red-checked handkerchief.

It's a dreadful job. Once it made sense. Out in the morning, lit at night. All day to rest, all night to sleep.
And the orders have changed?
They haven't! That's the trouble. Every year the planet turns faster, and the orders haven't changed!
Now it turns once a minute. I light and put out once a minute. I haven't a second's rest.
How funny! Your days last one minute!
It isn't funny. We've been talking for a month already.
A month?
Thirty minutes. Thirty days. Good evening.

The prince loved this man, so faithful to his orders. He wanted to help.

Your planet is so small. Just walk slowly and stay in the sun. The day will last as long as you like.
That doesn't help me much. What I love most is sleep.
That's bad luck.
It is. Good morning.
The others would look down on him. Yet he's the only one I don't find silly. Perhaps because he's thinking of something other than himself.

"He's the only one I could have made my friend. But his planet is too small. There's no room for two."

What he didn't dare admit was that he missed this planet most of all for its…

…one thousand four hundred and forty sunsets every twenty-four hours.

The GeographerChapter XV

The sixth planet was ten times bigger. An old gentleman lived there who wrote enormous books.

Well! An explorer!

The prince sat down and caught his breath. He had travelled so far.

What's that big book? What do you do?
I'm a geographer.
What's a geographer?
A scholar who knows where the seas, rivers, cities, mountains and deserts are.
How interesting. At last, a real profession!

He looked around. He had never seen so majestic a planet.

It's very beautiful. Does it have oceans?
I can't know that.
Oh. And mountains?
I can't know that.
Cities? Rivers? Deserts?
Nor that.
But you're a geographer!
Exactly. I'm not an explorer. A geographer is too important to go strolling about. He never leaves his desk.

He questions explorers, and looks into their morals. A liar would ruin his books. So would a drinker, who sees double.

I know someone who'd be a bad explorer.
Then you go and see?
Too complicated. The explorer must bring proof. For a big mountain, he must bring big stones.
But you! You come from far away! You're an explorer! Describe your planet!

He sharpened his pencil. Stories go down in pencil first. Ink must wait for proof.

Oh, it's not very interesting. It's tiny. I have three volcanoes. Two active, one extinct. But you never know.
You never know.
I also have a flower.
We don't record flowers.
Why not? She's the prettiest thing!
Because flowers are ephemeral.
What does 'ephemeral' mean?
Our books never go out of date. Mountains rarely move. Oceans rarely dry up. We write of eternal things.
But what does 'ephemeral' mean?
It means 'doomed to disappear soon'.
Is my flower doomed to disappear soon?
Of course.

"My flower is ephemeral. She has only four thorns to defend herself against the world. And I left her all alone."

It was the first time he regretted leaving.

But he took heart.

Where would you advise me to go?
The planet Earth. It has a good reputation.

And the prince set off, thinking of his flower.

End of Episode 5

What is it really saying?

Before opening these, decide for yourself what each chapter was telling you. Then compare. These are readings, and you're free to disagree with them.

Chapter XIII: the businessman

Owning something means being useful to it. Counting a thing is not the same as caring for it.

Where you see it
The slip of paper in the drawer, and the watering can with the answer he can't give.
What the author says
He counts the stars, owns them because he thought of it first, and uses his wealth to buy more. The prince says: my flower and my volcanoes gain from being mine. Your stars gain nothing from you.
Interpretation
It's a circle, like the drinker's: the thing is done for the sake of doing more of it. He can't even remember the word for what he counts. Compare the drawer in Episode 1, where a child's drawing was shut away.
A fair objection
Counting and money matter. Savings protect families, and someone has to keep accounts. The trouble is wealth with no purpose outside itself. And is "owning" the right word for love? A person isn't a possession, however well you water them.
Easy to miss
He boasts of being precise, yet in your edition he gives two different totals, 501,622,731 and then 501,622,730. Perhaps a joke, perhaps a slip in this edition.
Ask yourself: what do you count most carefully? Is it useful to anyone that you have it?
Chapter XIV: the lamplighter

Work has dignity when it serves something beyond yourself. But loyalty to a rule that no longer fits will wear you out.

Where you see it
Useful because it's beautiful, orders that haven't changed, and the flickering days.
What the author says
The planet speeds up every year and the orders stay the same. The prince finds him the only one who isn't silly, perhaps because he thinks of something other than himself. He could have been a friend, but there's no room for two.
Interpretation
Both halves are true at once. He is admirable and he is trapped. The world around him changed, nobody updated the rule, and he kept apologising with a handkerchief instead of questioning it. Many tired, loyal people would recognise this.
A fair objection
"Orders are orders" is also how people excuse terrible things, and the author wrote during a war. Being selfless doesn't make an order right. The prince offers a clever way out, yet it doesn't fit what the man needs, which is sleep. Help starts with asking.
Easy to miss
The prince isn't fully honest with himself either. He tells himself he regrets the friend. He also regrets the sunsets, and Chapter VI told us when he loves those.
Ask yourself: which rule are you still following although the planet has sped up? Who could change it, if not you?
Chapter XV: the geographer

You can know a great deal about the world and never meet it. And what doesn't last may matter most.

Where you see it
"I can't know that", "we don't record flowers", and the flower alone.
What the author says
He never leaves his desk. He records only what is eternal, and flowers are doomed to disappear soon. For the first time, the prince regrets leaving.
Interpretation
The geographer treats "it won't last" as a reason to leave something out. For the prince, the same fact is a reason to hurry back. One word gives him the fear that the whole journey has been missing. He didn't learn it from the wise man's wisdom, but from his indifference.
A fair objection
The geographer's caution is good science. He checks witnesses, asks for proof, and writes in pencil first. No one can see everything for themselves, and all of us rely on others' reports. His fault is that he's content never to look, even at his own planet. Even his belief that mountains don't change is wrong over long enough time.
Ask yourself: what in your life is ephemeral, and are you treating it as if it will always be there? What do you know only from a desk?
The thread through these three chapters, and all six planets

What is your work for? The king, the swank, the drinker and the businessman serve themselves. The lamplighter serves a lamp. The geographer serves a book.

Look back
Only the lamplighter thinks of something other than himself, and he's the one the prince would have stayed for. In each visit the prince asks, "What for?" The businessman and the drinker can only answer with a circle.
Watch for
He has now learned a frightening word and is going to Earth with it. See what happens to his idea of his flower.
About this manga
The businessman is drawn as the red-faced "serious man" of Chapter VII. They sound alike, and many readers connect them, but the book doesn't say they are the same. The sums on the wall in Episode 3 were borrowed from this chapter.
