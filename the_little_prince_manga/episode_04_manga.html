The KingChapter X

He found himself among asteroids 325 to 330. He began to visit them, for something to do and something to learn.

On the first lived a king in purple and ermine, seated on a simple but majestic throne.

Ah! A subject!
How can he know me? He has never seen me.

He didn't know that for kings the world is very simple. Everyone is a subject.

He looked for a place to sit, but the robe covered the whole planet. So he stood. And since he was tired, he yawned.

It's against etiquette to yawn before a king. I forbid it.
I can't help it. I've come a long way and I haven't slept.
Then I order you to yawn! I haven't seen a yawn in years. Come, again!
Now I'm shy. I can't.
Hum! Then I order you sometimes to yawn and sometimes to… erm…

He was cross. He would not be disobeyed. But he was also kind, so his orders were reasonable.

May I sit down?
I order you to sit.

And he drew in a fold of his great robe.

Sire… what do you rule over?
Everything.
And the stars obey you?
Of course. At once. I do not allow disobedience.

With such power, he thought, he could watch two hundred sunsets in a day and never move his chair. He thought of the little planet he'd left.

Please… would you order the sun to set?
If I ordered a general to fly from flower to flower like a butterfly, and he didn't, whose fault would it be?
Yours.
Exactly. Ask of each person only what they are able to give. Authority rests first of all on reason.
And my sunset?
You shall have it. I'll command it. But with my skill in government, I'll wait until conditions are favourable.
It will be… about… let me see… this evening. And you'll see how well I am obeyed!
I've nothing more to do here. I'm leaving.
Don't go! I'll make you a minister!
Of what?
Of… justice!
But there's nobody here to judge!
We can't be sure. I'm old, I can't walk far.

But the prince had already looked. Nobody on the other side either.

Then you shall judge yourself. That's the hardest of all. It's far harder to judge yourself than others. If you can do it well, you're truly wise.
I can judge myself anywhere. I don't need to live here for that.
There's an old rat here. Condemn it to death now and then, and pardon it each time. We have only the one.
I don't want to condemn anyone to death. I'm going.
No.
If Your Majesty would like to be obeyed, order me to leave within a minute. Conditions seem favourable…

The king didn't reply. The prince hesitated, sighed, and left.

I make you my ambassador!
Grown-ups really are peculiar.

The SwankChapter XI

On the second planet lived a swank, a man in love with himself.

Ah! Here comes an admirer!

For to the vain, everyone else is an admirer.

Hello. That's a funny hat.
It's for raising when people cheer me. Unfortunately, nobody ever comes this way.
Clap your hands together.

The prince clapped. The swank raised his hat modestly.

"This is more fun than the king," thought the prince. He clapped again. Up went the hat. Again. Up went the hat. After five minutes, he was bored.

What must I do to make the hat fall off?

The swank didn't hear. The vain hear nothing but praise.

Do you really admire me very much?
What does 'admire' mean?
It means you know that I'm the handsomest, best-dressed, richest and cleverest man on this planet.
But you're the only man on your planet!
Do me this kindness. Admire me all the same!
I admire you. But why should that interest you so much?
Grown-ups really are very peculiar.

The DrinkerChapter XII

On the next planet lived a drinker. It was a very short visit, but it left the prince deeply sad.

What are you doing?
Drinking.
Why are you drinking?
To forget.
To forget what?
To forget that I'm ashamed.

He hung his head.

The prince wanted to help.

Ashamed of what?
Ashamed of drinking.

And he shut himself up in silence.

The prince went away, puzzled.

Grown-ups really are very, very peculiar.

End of Episode 4

What is it really saying?

Before opening these, decide for yourself what each chapter was telling you. Then compare. These are readings, and you're free to disagree with them.

Chapter X: the king

Real authority asks only what people are able to give. The hardest person to judge fairly is yourself.

Where you see it
Authority and reason, the sunset at 7.40, and judging yourself.
What the author says
To kings, everyone is a subject. This one won't bear disobedience, but he is kind, so he gives only reasonable orders. He is proud to finally be a king for someone.
Interpretation
He commands only what would happen anyway: yawns, sitting down, the evening sun. It keeps the feeling of power safe from the truth. Behind it is an old man alone with a rat, offering titles so that a child will stay.
A fair objection
Don't laugh at him too quickly. "Ask only what people can give" is wise advice for any parent, teacher or manager. Also notice what he leaves out: a good leader should help people give more, and sometimes change conditions instead of waiting for them.
Easy to miss
The prince leaves kindly. He uses the king's own rule so that the old man can feel obeyed. He has become gentler since the quarrel over the thorns.
Ask yourself: do you ever take credit for something that would have happened anyway? And when you judge yourself, are you fair, or only harsh or only lenient?
Chapter XI: the swank

If you need applause to feel real, you stop hearing everything else. And praise you have to beg for is worth nothing.

Where you see it
A hat for crowds that never come, the question he can't hear, and "admire me all the same".
What the author says
Vain people see only admirers and hear only praise. He wants to be called the best man on a planet where he is the only man.
Interpretation
Admiration needs no knowledge of you. The prince claps without knowing why, and the swank is content. It also gets dull in five minutes, for the one giving it. Today it's easy to think of likes and follower counts, though that comparison is mine.
A fair objection
Wanting to be recognised is human, and people starved of it aren't simply foolish. Compare him with the flower. She was vain too, and the prince loved her. She gave something, her perfume and her light. He only takes.
A word about the word
"Swank" is this translator's choice. Other versions say "conceited man" or "vain man".
Ask yourself: whose applause are you working for? Would you still do the work if nobody could see it?
Chapter XII: the drinker

Shame can become its own cause. You do the thing to escape the feeling that the thing gives you.

Where you see it
The ring. The book gives only the conversation. Drawing it as a circle is this manga's way of showing it.
What the author says
He drinks to forget that he is ashamed of drinking. The visit is very short, and it depresses the prince, who pities him and wants to help.
Interpretation
The shape is wider than alcohol: avoiding work because you feel guilty for avoiding it, scrolling to escape the feeling of a wasted day. It can't be broken from inside by more shame. It needs a break somewhere: one honest conversation, one person, one different action.
A fair objection
The chapter is neat, and real addiction isn't. It has causes in the body, in pain and in circumstances, and people do recover, often with help. Note too that the prince asks good questions, then leaves. Understanding someone isn't yet helping them, though he is only a child.
Easy to miss
Of the three men, this is the only one who tells the plain truth about himself. That's why this manga draws him without the blank spectacles and hat that the others wear.
Ask yourself: do you have a small circle like this? Where is the easiest point to step out of it?
The thread through these three chapters

Each man is alone and fills the space with something: subjects, applause, a bottle. None of them asks the prince who he is.

Look back
In Chapter IV the narrator said grown-ups never ask what matters about a friend. Here are three who don't ask anything about him. The flower also wanted attention, but she said "I love you" in the end.
A caution
The refrain grows each time: peculiar, very peculiar, very, very peculiar. The prince is judging too, and quickly. Keep the king's line about judging yourself in mind as he travels on.
A detail in your edition
Chapter VI says forty-three sunsets, but Chapter X of the same book says forty-four. Versions of the book differ on this number, and your edition uses both.
